## Design Context

### Users
EleutherIA is primarily for professional scholars, doctoral researchers,
classicists, historians of philosophy, patristics specialists, and advanced
students. They use it during long, mostly desktop research sessions to trace an
argument across ancient witnesses, editions, translations, and modern
interpretations; test a historiographical claim; inspect counter-evidence; and
leave with a citation-ready, reproducible path back to the source.

### Brand Personality
Erudite, immersive, limpid. The interface should create confidence first,
intellectual wonder second, and never anxiety or spectacle for its own sake.
It should feel like a living critical atlas: authoritative enough for a
publication workflow, but inviting enough to reward exploration.

Fluidity is part of the brand promise. The product should feel closer to a
carefully directed AAA strategy game than to a conventional dashboard:
immediate input response, stable camera motion, coherent spatial transitions,
and adaptive visual quality that never sacrifices legibility.

### Chosen Direction
- Intent: A luminous intellectual atlas where a scholar can move from a panoramic map of the debate to the exact textual proof without ever losing the thread.
- Type pairing: Instrument Serif for editorial landmarks and major titles; DM Sans for controls, navigation, and dense metadata; EB Garamond for ancient texts, translations, and quotation-led reading surfaces.
- Palette seed: EleutherIA's existing painted world - parchment `#FCF9F4`, ink `#1C1917`, terracotta `#C2410C`, manuscript amber `#D97706`, olive/sage accents, and deep Aegean blue reserved for spatial depth and selected-state contrast.
- Motion language: cartographic, restrained, precise.
- Signature device: the Evidence Thread - selecting a node reveals one persistent luminous route from claim to ancient locus, edition, translation, and competing modern interpretations, with every hop inspectable and citable.

The atlas has three synchronized working modes rather than one overloaded
canvas: **Atlas** for spatial Evidence Flight, **Chronos** for temporal and
transmission views, and **Scholar** for dense comparison, parallel sources,
filters, notes, and exports. Switching modes must preserve the selected nodes,
filters, intellectual path, and evidence context.

### Design Principles
1. Proof before decoration: every impressive visual gesture must clarify an intellectual relation, source, or state.
2. Panorama to locus: preserve orientation while progressively disclosing detail, down to page, line, CTS URN, and quotation.
3. Scholar control: filters, provenance, comparison, export, history, and undo remain visible or one deliberate step away.
4. Light is the working environment: use dark Aegean depth locally for spatial focus, never as the default reading surface.
5. Complexity is structured, not hidden: compress and stage the 20k-node graph without amputating the evidence scholars need.
6. Performance is a feature: target a 16.7 ms frame budget on representative mid-range hardware, treat 8.3 ms as the high-end stretch target, keep interaction work off the main thread where practical, and degrade effects before dropping clarity or control.
7. One extraordinary moment at a time: cinematic graph motion must serve navigation and provenance; reading, filtering, comparison, and citation surfaces remain calm.
