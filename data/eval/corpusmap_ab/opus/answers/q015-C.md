The Augustine–Pelagius controversy (c. 411–431 CE) is presented in the KG as the foundational Western dispute over grace, free will and original sin [debate_augustine_pelagius_grace].

Pelagius, reacting against moral laxity excused by appeals to human weakness, stressed natural human capacity, moral responsibility and perfectionism; his position was condemned at Carthage (418) and Ephesus (431) [argument_pelagiuss_argument_for_human_capacity_006c0a58]. Scheck traces his Romans exegesis on free choice and foreknowledge to Origen's anti-determinism, and cautions that his silence about actual grace in those glosses is not by itself an explicit denial of grace [scholarly_argument_scheck_pelagius_receives_origen_free_choice_with_historical_caveat].

Against Pelagius, Caelestius and Julian of Eclanum, Augustine argued that grace is necessary. The KG describes this as a move away from his early optimism about free choice and towards a position stressing human depravity [argument_augustines_antipelagian_argument_grace_necessity_0b29401f]. Dihle (1982) reads Augustine's voluntas as shaped by two fronts. Against the Manichaeans the will had to be free, so that evil comes from the will's defect. Against the Pelagians it had to be wounded by original sin and in need of prevenient grace [argument_dihle_1982_augustine_responds_to_manichean_and_pelagian].

The primary texts show three points:
- Augustine wrote De gratia et libero arbitrio for the monks of Hadrumetum. It targets people who defend free choice so strongly that they deny grace and say it is given "secundum merita nostra" [dc656ba2-f862-483f-979a-448db889764a].
- In the Retractationes, Augustine says 14 bishops in Palestine declared Pelagius catholic only because he condemned the dogmas "inimica gratiae Christi" [680d9ce3-3a2a-401f-b20a-fd8e8b2caad9].
- De correptione et gratia says that mercy comes before a person, with no prior merits. Even eternal life is grace, given "pro his meritis quae contulit gratia" [7c999924-6095-4b8c-96b2-21207675a8b4].

The corpus holds no passages by Pelagius himself.
