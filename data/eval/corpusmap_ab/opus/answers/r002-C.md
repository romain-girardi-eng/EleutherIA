ANSWER:
Two distinct works and transmissions. De Principiis III.1 (the treatise "On Freedom") survives in Greek in the Philocalia and in Rufinus' Latin [argument_furst_2022_de_princ_iii_1_first_freedom_treatise]. The anti-astrological dissertation belongs to the Commentary on Genesis III (c. 220, after De Principiis), excerpted in Philocalia 23 and transcribed almost verbatim by Eusebius, Praep. Ev. VI.11 [argument_origen_anti_astrological].

De Princ. III.1.5–6: the corpus offers only French translations, not Greek or Latin witnesses. One follows the De Principiis line [ae853539-5bf0-592e-9323-f4dad81d7fc8; 5d4a53e1-3e27-5179-98b4-1aa1231218f3]. The other is labelled Philocalia 21.5, the Greek-transmitted text [a4f7926d-487c-5ab4-98a5-a5f2736788e8]. The argument is psychological and pedagogical. Blaming external stimuli makes us like "bois et pierres" moved from outside. Blaming our constitution goes against the evidence of education, which reforms even the most intemperate. Section 6 then adds scriptural proof: Micah, Moses ("choisis le bien") and Isaiah show that living well or badly is "notre oeuvre", not forced by "destins" [5d4a53e1-3e27-5179-98b4-1aa1231218f3].

Philocalia 23.1: the corpus has no Philocalia 23 passage. Its Greek is attested through Eusebius, PE VI.11.2 [7c8ea1fb-d866-52b9-b66e-4b9a18f042fe], and through Amand's quotation [argument_origen_witness_carneades_transposition_praise_blame_amand1945]. Here the argument is a Carneadean reductio: astral determinism "abolishes τὸ ἐφ' ἡμῖν", and with it praise, blame and accountable actions. Origen gives this an eschatological turn, since God's announced judgement, threats and rewards would then disappear [argument_origen_witness_carneades_transposition_praise_blame_amand1945]. The wider dissertation adds that stars are signs, not causes [argument_origen_anti_astrological].

Contrast: III.1.5–6 argues positively from experience and Scripture. Phil. 23.1 argues by consequence against a named opponent, astrology.
