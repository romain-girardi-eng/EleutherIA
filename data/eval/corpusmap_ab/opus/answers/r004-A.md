The EleutherIA corpus does not attest the text of Tertullian, De Anima 21, nor any Waszink commentary on it. The KG has a work node for De Anima (c. 210–213 CE, ed. Waszink, CCSL 2; 58 chapters, the earliest systematic Latin treatise on the soul, engaging Stoic, Platonic and medical sources), but it explicitly records that no passages from this work are in the database: 31 passages once attributed to it actually come from Adversus Praxean and have been reassigned [work_tertullian_de_anima]. Its only graph edge is authorship by Tertullian [work_tertullian_de_anima, person_tertullian_d220]. Passage searches for the chapter's themes (free choice, mutability of nature, Stoic fate) returned only Augustine, Evodius, Cassian and Boethius, and no Tertullian text.

What the KG does say about Tertullian's position is general. De Anima sets out a traducian theory of the soul, with consequences for inherited moral corruption and the scope of responsibility. Adversus Marcionem II.5–7 defends liberum arbitrium: if a good God created humanity, evil must come from human choice [person_tertullian_d220]. Modern scholars describe this anti-Marcionite theodicy as libertarian [argument_tertullians_antimarcionite_argument_for_free_will_f49cad73]. No node links Tertullian or De Anima to a critique of Stoic determinism, and no node for Waszink's commentary exists, apart from the edition named in the work description.

So the specific claim (De Anima 21 against Stoic determinism, as Waszink reads it) cannot be checked from this corpus and should be treated as unknown here.
