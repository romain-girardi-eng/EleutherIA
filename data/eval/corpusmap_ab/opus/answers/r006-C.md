The KG attests the Pseudo-Clementine Recognitiones as ten books surviving in Rufinus' Latin (c. 406 CE). The lost Greek original dates to c. 220–260 in Syria and shares a lost Grundschrift with the Homiliae [work_ps_clement_recognitiones; person_pseudo_clement; work_ps_clement_homiliae]. The question needs one correction. In the KG, Recogn. III (the Caesarea disputations) holds Peter's anti-Simonian case for free will. Peter grounds moral responsibility in autexousion against Simon's astrological "genesis" [work_ps_clement_recognitiones; person_pseudo_clement]. The full anti-astrological dialogue is not in Book III. It sits in Books VIII–X (Laodicea), especially IX.19–29, where Clement's father argues that all things are subject to genesis. The Homilies parallel is Hom. XIV.3–11 [work_ps_clement_recognitiones; work_ps_clement_homiliae].
