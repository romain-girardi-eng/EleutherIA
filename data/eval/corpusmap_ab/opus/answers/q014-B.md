In the corpus, "intellectualism vs voluntarism" is a medieval distinction projected back onto antiquity [debate_intellectualism_vs_voluntarism_w3x4y5z6]. Medieval voluntarism (Scotus, Ockham) holds that the will can choose against the intellect's judgment of the best, a self-determining power [concept_voluntarism_medieval_m7n8o9p0; argument_scotuss_voluntarism_501f1bf6; argument_ockhams_rejection_of_necessitation_33c255bf].

Ancient ethics starts from Socratic intellectualism: virtue is knowledge and no one does wrong willingly [concept_socratic_intellectualism_f6g7h8i9], so action reduces to cognition of the good [synthesis_dihle1982_lec2_greek_intellectualism_action]. Epictetus keeps this: no one assents to what seems false, and every soul is deprived of truth unwillingly [d870c668-956a-5fb0-81b4-61f03a62e102; 347cf7bb-095e-53a3-ade7-a51a4191856e].

Aristotle grounds responsibility in voluntary action (origin in the agent, knowledge of particulars) [argument_voluntary_involuntary_distinction_aristotle_g7h8i9j0]. He holds that acting and not acting are both "up to us" [1e336c5e-391c-5613-a59d-b83d2bcc5523], and that we are partly responsible for how the good appears to us [28b16b62-34cb-4db0-a445-c778d696cb4e]. Dihle says prohairesis and boulesis still presuppose cognition, and that akrasia is a failure of cognition [synthesis_dihle1982_lec2_greek_intellectualism_action].

Dihle's thesis is that the Greeks never developed a distinct concept of will [argument_dihle_1982_greek_intellectualism_thesis; pub_dihle_1982_theory_of_will]. He reads Stoic synkatathesis as "voluntary, yet necessitated" and still cognitive [argument_dihle_1982_synkatathesis_is_cognitive_not_volitional]. He calls ascribing a will to the Greeks anachronistic [argument_dihle_1982_critique_anachronism_in_attribution_will_to_greeks]. Voelke rejects the either/or itself: in Stoic rationalism, reason is will [scholarly_argument_voelke_stoic_reason_is_will]. A will that conflicts with itself appears with Augustine [312d556f-781c-574d-801e-3a9c348ae516].
