In Strom. II Clement takes over the Stoic psychology of assent (συγκατάθεσις) and puts it to work against Gnostic "natural" determinism [argument_clement_grace_synergy_assent; work_clement_stromateis].

Stoic material: he defines faith as "πρόληψις ἑκούσιος… θεοσεβείας συγκατάθεσις" [408ca550-86bf-5921-8b09-33dbbeaf5aa3]. He says that Platonists and Stoics alike place assents "ἐφ' ἡμῖν" [4031dce4-c46f-5731-b3ed-bed07228525d]. Every opinion, judgement and act of learning is an assent, and unbelief, as apostasy from faith, shows that assent is a real possibility [17d043b5-0b2c-55d1-b92e-6e6e05055c13]. The Logos calls everyone, "ὅτι ἐφ' ἡμῖν τὸ πείθεσθαι", and the people drawn respond "οὐχ ὡς αἴτια, ἀλλ' ὡς συναίτια" [8a7158d8-b842-5b2f-8cb6-d9ac68d184d1]. Löhr links this vocabulary to Chrysippus' distinction between internal and external causes [scholarly_argument_l_hr_clement_s_use_of_stoic_concept_3].

Christian shift: the target is belief and unbelief rather than virtue and vice. If faith were "φύσεως πλεονέκτημα", there could be no just reward, praise or blame. We would be moved like puppets (νευροσπαστουμένων), and repentance and baptism would make no sense [5a221307-b0c5-5f9a-87b3-771fbd2f312b]. Amand reads this as an argument from Carneades with Christian terms swapped in [argument_clement_alex_carneadean_glissement_faith_unbelief]. Responsibility also becomes a cooperation (synergy) with grace: God draws the soul without forcing it [argument_clement_grace_synergy_assent]. Ewing adds that Clement rejects the Stoic identification of providence with fate [scholarly_argument_ewing_stoic_pervasion].

Scholars question how well this works. Löhr calls the argument weak, because Basilides also defined faith as assent [scholarly_argument_l_hr_clement_s_use_of_stoic_concept_3]. Karfíková doubts whether the rejected alternative is really "equally possible" [scholarly_argument_karfikova_autexousion_prerequisite_not_gnostic_freedom]. Müller argues that Clement goes beyond Stoic freedom from the passions (apatheia) by making love the basis of freedom [scholarly_argument_mueller_clement_love_surpasses_apatheia_as_freedom].
