A correction to the premise first. According to the knowledge graph, Recognitions III (the Caesarea disputations) is not the anti-astrological dialogue. In Book III Peter argues against Simon's astrological determinism by grounding moral responsibility in human autexousion. The elaborate anti-astrological apologetic belongs to Recogn. VIII–X (Laodicea), especially IX.19–29: Clement's debate on genesis with his father Faustus/Faustinianus [work_ps_clement_recognitiones].

Transmission: ten books, extant in Rufinus' Latin (c. 406); the Greek is lost. Composed c. 220–260 in Syria [work_ps_clement_recognitiones]. The Homilies give the parallel debate at Hom. XIV.3–11. Both derive from a lost Grundschrift [work_ps_clement_homiliae].

Sources:
(1) Bardaisan's Book of the Laws of the Countries. The Recognitions take over its argument from the diversity of national laws. The KG records Pseudo-Clement as influenced by Bardaisan. Apart from Eusebius, PE VI.10, this is the fullest Patristic reuse of the argument [person_pseudo_clement; person_bardesanes_the_syrian_3r8s0u76; argument_bardaisan_nomima_barbarika; work_bardaisan_book_of_laws]. The Homilies node notes that "nomima barbarika" is strictly a Greek book-title, and the Recognitions version is better called the "laws of the countries" [work_ps_clement_homiliae].
(2) Origen, Philocalia 23 quotes the Clement–father exchange from book 14 of the Periodoi, almost word for word as in the Recognitions. Amand (1945) infers that both texts depend on an earlier Christian polemic against astrology [argument_origen_witness_diss_part3_pseudo_clementine_amand1945; passage_origen_philocalia_23_22]. That passage attacks astrologers' post-hoc explanations and "climacteric years", and appeals to free reason (French translation only).
