ANSWER:
Two distinct works and transmissions. De Principiis III.1 (Peri autexousiou) survives in Greek in Philocalia ch. 21 and in Rufinus' Latin [argument_furst_2022_de_princ_iii_1_first_freedom_treatise; sc268_origenes_peri_archon]. The anti-astrological dissertation belongs to the Commentary on Genesis III (c. 220, after De Principiis), transmitted in Philocalia 23 and nearly verbatim in Eusebius, PE VI.11 [argument_origen_anti_astrological].

De Princ. III.1.5–6: the corpus holds only French translations, not Greek or Latin witnesses. These are two differently worded French renderings, one under De Princ. 3.1.5–6 [ae853539-5bf0-592e-9323-f4dad81d7fc8; 5d4a53e1-3e27-5179-98b4-1aa1231218f3] and one under Philocalia 21.5 [a4f7926d-487c-5ab4-98a5-a5f2736788e8]. The argument is about blame: shifting it to external events makes us like "bois et pierres" moved from outside. Education reforms even the intemperate, so natural constitution does not excuse us [ae853539…; a4f7926d…]. Section 6 confirms by Scripture (Micah, Moses, Isaiah) that living well or badly is "notre oeuvre", not forced by events or destinies [5d4a53e1-3e27-5179-98b4-1aa1231218f3].

Philocalia 23.1: no passage of it is in the corpus. Its Greek is known only through Amand's quotation: astral fatalism abolishes τὸ ἐφ᾽ ἡμῖν, and with it praise, blame and God's announced judgement, punishments and rewards [argument_origen_witness_carneades_transposition_praise_blame_amand1945]. It is a Carneadean consequence argument recast for eschatology. The wider dissertation treats the stars as signs, not causes [argument_origen_anti_astrological; scholarly_argument_nascimento_stars_are_signs_not_causes].

Comparison: III.1.5–6 argues from the agent's own psychology and from Scripture. Phil. 23.1 argues from the destructive consequences of a fatalist thesis. Both defend responsibility.
