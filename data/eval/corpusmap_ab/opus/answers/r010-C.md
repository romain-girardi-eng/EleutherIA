The Treatise of the Two Spirits (1QS III.13–IV.26), embedded in the Community Rule, is the most explicit predestinarian text of Second Temple Judaism: "From the God of Knowledge comes all that is and shall be" (III.15); God created a Spirit of Truth and a Spirit of Injustice and assigned each person to one at creation, with sin attributed to the Angel of Darkness [argument_qumran_predestination_c3d4e5f6; work_dss_community_rule_e7f8g9h0; concept_two_spirits_doctrine_s7t8u9v0]. Scholars disagree on its rigidity: Lange reads strict double predestination "comparable to later Calvinist formulations", Frey a modified dualism leaving room for decision [argument_qumran_predestination_c3d4e5f6]. Brand sees the Treatise fusing predetermination with an internal struggle of two spirits in every heart [scholarly_argument_brand_two_spirits_synthesizes_views], and argues free-will views vary by genre rather than forming one "Qumran theology" [scholarly_argument_brand_genre_determines_free_will_view]. Klawans links it to Josephus's Essene determinism [scholarly_argument_klawans_2012_essene_determinism_matches_qumran].

The KG's documented bridge to early Christianity is the Two Ways tradition: Prigent notes that Barnabas 18–20 (way of light / way of darkness) reuses a Jewish moral manual paralleled in Didache 1–6 and 1QS 3,13–4,26 [scholarly_argument_prigent_free_will_and_moral_choice_in__0; work_didache; sc172_epistula_barnabae]. But Christian use is paraenetic: Justin makes angels and humans αὐτεξούσιοι [7b029b3b-d680-4362-a0ea-8ef4b2aef215]; Origen attacks fixed "natures" as destroying prohairesis [scholarly_argument_sytsma_anti_determinism_idle_argument_deprinc_iii]. Augustine's predestination of the elect returns to a structurally comparable position, rooted in Romans 9 [concept_predestination_augustinian; debate_augustine_pelagius_grace]. The corpus records no direct historical dependence of Patristic predestination on 1QS, and holds no 1QS passages themselves.
