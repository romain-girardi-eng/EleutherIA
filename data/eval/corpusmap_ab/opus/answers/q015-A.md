The KG treats the Augustine-Pelagius dispute (411-431 CE) as the foundational Western controversy on grace, free will and original sin [debate_augustine_pelagius_grace], with both as participants [person_augustine_hippo_d430][person_pelagius_d420].

Pelagius: human nature carries a divinely implanted capacity (posse, the naturale bonum), while willing (velle) and doing (esse) remain the agent's own; grace assists through forgiveness, Scripture and Christ's example but does not determine the will [person_pelagius_d420]. He read Romans 5:12 as moral imitation of Adam, not inherited guilt [person_pelagius_d420]; his Romans exegesis drew on Origen's anti-determinist reading [scholarly_argument_scheck_pelagius_receives_origen_free_choice_with_historical_caveat] and grounded predestination in foreknowledge [scholarly_argument_matteoli_divine_prescience_and_free_wil_1]. Julian of Eclanum later systematized Pelagian views against Augustine [person_julian_eclanum_d454].

Augustine: original sin corrupts nature universally; without grace the will cannot avoid sin [debate_augustine_pelagius_grace]. Humanity is a massa damnata from which God elects gratuitously [argument_augustine_massa_damnata]; final perseverance is donum, not meritum [argument_augustine_donum_perseverantiae]. In the corpus he contrasts Adam's "posse si vellet" with the later gift of grace and the distinction "posse non peccare"/"non posse peccare" [aaeed4fb-f6c0-476f-a70c-7f7da9383b3d]; he says grace precedes without prior merits [7c999924-6095-4b8c-96b2-21207675a8b4] and frees from sin where the law only commands [eb274f08-00b4-4373-8ba8-d3a5e46d4ffa]. He wrote De gratia et libero arbitrio against those who deny grace by claiming it is given "secundum merita nostra" [dc656ba2-f862-483f-979a-448db889764a], and denied that his earlier pro-free-will statements favoured the "noui heretici Pelagiani" [0173853c-a857-4ab7-8b7f-46d85724cc08].

According to the KG, Pelagius was condemned at Carthage (418) and the controversy settled at Ephesus (431), with Orange (529) later moderating Augustinian extremes [debate_augustine_pelagius_grace][person_pelagius_d420]. No Pelagius texts are attested as passages in the corpus.
