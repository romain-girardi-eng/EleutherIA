Two distinct works and transmissions. De Principiis survives in Rufinus' Latin, but III.1 is preserved in Greek in Philocalia ch. 21, compiled c. 358-359 by Basil and Gregory of Nazianzus [work_de_principiis_origen_230s_v2w3x4y5][work_origen_philocalia][sc268_origenes_peri_archon]. The Commentary on Genesis (13 books, c. 230) is lost in direct tradition; a Book III fragment survives in Greek as Philocalia ch. 23, "On Fate" (SC 226) [work_origen_commentary_genesis].

De Princ. III.1.5: the Greek [5c8ff8f7-8ca3-5853-89b6-46474854f639] rejects blaming externals and likening ourselves to "wood and stones" moved from outside; blaming bare constitution is refuted by education transforming the most intemperate. Two French corpus translations exist: one rendering the Philocalia Greek (21.5) [a4f7926d-487c-5ab4-98a5-a5f2736788e8], another of the De Principiis text ("liberté de la volonté") [ae853539-5bf0-592e-9323-f4dad81d7fc8]; both are modern translations, not witnesses. Background: the external/internal distinction at III.1.3 [3e767176-490c-58a3-a173-c7e10e5d85a9][11748b4e-d973-58b0-bc50-fdcad4cddfca]. I retrieved no passage for III.1.6; Fürst links it to anti-Gnostic "natures" determinism [argument_furst_2022_origen_against_three_determinisms].

Philocalia 23.1: the corpus holds no passage (Greek or translation) for it [work_origen_commentary_genesis], so its wording cannot be quoted here. The KG only records it as anti-astrological polemic central to the Carneadean tradition (Amand), and Fürst classes it against popular astrological determinism, distinct from the Stoic (III.1.4ff.) and Gnostic (III.1.6) fronts [argument_furst_2022_origen_against_three_determinisms][pub_hall_2020_origen_and_astrology].

Comparison: III.1.5 grounds responsibility in assent and educability against external/constitutional excuses. The Genesis fragment targets stellar causation, but any detailed comparison of its arguments is not attested in the corpus.
