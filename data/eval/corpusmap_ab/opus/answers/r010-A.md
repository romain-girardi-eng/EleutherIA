The KG treats the Treatise of the Two Spirits (1QS III.13–IV.26) as Second Temple Judaism's most explicit predestinarian text: God fixes "their whole design" before creation (III.15) and assigns each person to the Spirit of Truth or of Injustice, under the Prince of Light / Angel of Darkness, until the appointed end [argument_qumran_predestination_c3d4e5f6; work_dss_community_rule_e7f8g9h0]. Scholars disagree on its rigidity. Lange reads it as strict double predestination, and Frey as a modified dualism [argument_qumran_predestination_c3d4e5f6]. Klawans equates it with Josephus' Essene "Fate" [scholarly_argument_klawans_2012_essene_determinism_matches_qumran]. Brand sees it combining predestination with an inner struggle of the two spirits [scholarly_argument_brand_two_spirits_synthesizes_views], and the 1QS recension stressing free choice [scholarly_argument_brand_covenantal_texts_free_choice].

On the Patristic side, Augustine's predestination (election plus passing over the rest) [concept_predestination_augustinian] rests on the massa damnata argument from Romans 9 (Ad Simplicianum I.2) [argument_augustine_massa_damnata]. It differs from 1QS: reprobation follows Adam's fall, not a created assignment. Gnostic (Greek) and Manichaean (Latin) readers shaped the Patristic predestinarian reading of Romans 9 [scholarly_argument_moon_patristic_predestinarianism_an_0], and Origen worked out his predestination against gnostic thinkers [scholarly_argument_belcastro_predestination_as_prescience_v_3]. Hermas offers repentance to all [5410130e-86a7-51cf-9082-a6d3454a0155]. Eskola warns that Calvinist determinism has coloured readings of Second Temple texts [scholarly_argument_eskola_double_predestination_3].
