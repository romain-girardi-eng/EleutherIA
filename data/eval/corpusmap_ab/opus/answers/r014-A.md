Coverage is uneven. The KG has no node for Käsemann, and no passage mentions him (searches for "Käsemann" returned nothing relevant). His position therefore cannot be stated from the corpus. Barclay's *Paul and the Gift* (2015) has no node of its own either. It appears only as the work that *Paul and the Power of Grace* is a sequel to [pub_barclay_2020_power_of_grace].

Barclay's view as the KG records it:
- He notes that the grace/works antithesis (Rom 4.4-6; 9.6-13; 11.6) made Paul the main spokesman for God's unconditional initiative. That reading encouraged polemics against any human contribution, and he proposes a comparative approach instead [scholarly_argument_barclay_divine_grace_and_human_works_3; scholarly_work_barclay_2006_divine_and_human_agency_in_paul_and_his_].
- Grace is "incongruous": given without regard to worth, it creates worth [scholarly_argument_barclay_2020_incongruous_gift]. It is one of six separable "perfections" [scholarly_argument_barclay_2020_six_perfections].
- Grace is "unconditioned" (no prior worth) but not "unconditional", because it expects to change the person who receives it [scholarly_argument_barclay_2020_unconditioned_not_unconditional]. The KG links this node to synergism and grace–freedom synergy [concept_synergism; concept_grace_freedom_synergy]. Its linked texts include Rom 5:6 and 2 Cor 5:10 [748548af-236a-59be-aca4-ff36f4a28dc4; fd238a34-57d1-546c-add4-6f30d66d01b7].
- Barclay also says grace can range from helping human agency to replacing it [scholarly_argument_barclay_2020_efficacy_spectrum_agency].

The text itself: Rom 9:16, "οὐ τοῦ θέλοντος οὐδὲ τοῦ τρέχοντος ἀλλὰ τοῦ ἐλεῶντος θεοῦ" [f4a08094-ca02-599f-a80e-ec4528bdd3a1; a81c7f21-42a0-5d76-b89e-a0a07c8a1a30]. Origen reads the verse synergistically, against predestinarian objectors [efee04cb-a2d3-5465-bada-431ca4b2c6e4; bbe7d2ef-2550-5aed-b42b-c58ee46f153f].
