A correction first: the KG does not put the anti-astrological dialogue in Recognitions Book III. Book III (the disputations at Caesarea) holds Peter's arguments against Simon. Peter grounds moral responsibility in human autexousion, against Simon's astrological determinism. The full anti-astrological apologetic is in Books VIII–X (Laodicea), especially IX.19–29. There a "Christian philosopher" argues against genesis, i.e. destiny set by the planets [work_ps_clement_recognitiones]. The work survives in Rufinus' Latin (c. 406). The Greek original, composed c. 220–260 in Syria, is lost [work_ps_clement_recognitiones; person_pseudo_clement].

Sources: the disputation draws on Bardaisan's Book of the Laws of the Countries. It came through the lost Grundschrift that the Recognitions share with the Homilies [work_ps_clement_recognitiones; person_pseudo_clement → influenced_by Bardaisan]. Its core is the nomima barbarika argument. National customs differ by people, not by birth-hour, so stars do not determine moral conduct [argument_bardaisan_nomima_barbarika]. Amand counts Recogn. IX.19–28, with Eusebius PE VI.10 and Pseudo-Caesarius, as derived from the Greek translation of the Book of the Laws. He traces the argument back through Bardaisan to Carneades [synthesis_amand1945_bardesanes_secondary_witness_ethnographic; debate_carneadean_antiastrology_tradition]. For the parallel Greek text see Eusebius PE VI.10 [3cb326d1-edb6-5302-b756-a273e8a72752; 71a9f18e-2ec5-5e0a-bd8b-440880141b1d].

Second witness: Origen's Philocalia 23 quotes Clement's reply to his father Faustus on genesis, from book 14 of the Periodoi. The quote matches the Recognitions passage almost word for word (PG 1, 1425A–1427B). Amand concludes that both depend on an earlier Christian anti-astrological polemic [argument_origen_witness_diss_part3_pseudo_clementine_amand1945].
