On the corpus evidence, this looks mostly like a clarification Augustine made himself, though some real shift in emphasis remains. Caveat: the Latin of De lib. arb. III.18-21 is not in the passage corpus. It is known here only through the KG synthesis and Augustine's own quotations in the Retractationes.

Pre-Pelagian position: III.20-22 separates "natural" ignorance and difficulty, which are blameless and the soul's starting point for progress, from "penal" ignorance and difficulty, which follow from voluntary neglect [synthesis_aug_natural_penal]. Book III traces this inherited weakness to Adam's fall [work_de_libero_arbitrio]. The treatise was aimed at Manichaean dualism [argument_augustines_early_antimanichean_argument_de_libero_arbitrio_debb866c; 818a6c6e-7f24-4e5f-94df-07cb8997292f].

Post-Pelagian reading: Augustine admits Pelagius used his line "quis enim peccat in eo, quod nullo modo cauere potest?" He then cites what follows it in Book III: acts done in ignorance or by necessity are also blamed (Rom 7; Gal 5) [561d0edd-4bc6-4fe3-9efb-f99715287c1b]. He says grace went unmentioned only because it was not the subject, and that the will cannot live rightly unless prevenient grace frees it [75ab6db0-fb86-49d2-a510-f90b7c3b8bf4; 0173853c-a857-4ab7-8b7f-46d85724cc08]. He claims the books argued "as if already against them": man could fall by free will but could not rise without grace [4a65bea1-32ed-4bfb-8acc-022c64ce3b98]. "No sin unless voluntary" is narrowed to sin that is not also a penalty of sin [3a06e7e9-ad0b-41ec-8ac9-f8530e4ca896; 527d4f7a-1fee-477e-8166-c5a5aabd6470].

Assessment: Book III already contains the theory of penal ignorance and difficulty. But the Retractationes add restrictions that are not in the early wording, notably "in tantum libera, in quantum liberata" [527d4f7a-1fee-477e-8166-c5a5aabd6470] and prevenient grace. So the shift is real in emphasis and in how far the will can act alone. Augustine presents it as continuity. That is his own self-interpretation, and the corpus offers no independent check on it.
