Chrysippus: fate is an eternal, unbreakable natural concatenation in which each thing follows on another (Gellius NA 7.2.3) [ad59899c-4c0e-4d0b-8825-6ee05e34fb19]. Nothing happens without antecedent causes. He escapes necessity by splitting causes into "perfect and principal" ones and "auxiliary and proximate" ones. Fate works through the proximate causes only (Cic. Fat. 41) [fc50aff8-32e5-443b-950d-35ad89b15d96]. The impression is the proximate cause of assent but does not necessitate it (Fat. 44) [64cca2fc-2fe6-466e-8443-e836e02bcc83]. In the cylinder analogy, an external push starts the motion and the thing's own nature does the rest. The cylinder's moral force is disputed [concept_cylinder_analogy_chrysippus_e5f6g7h8].

Alexander rejects this whole picture of causation:
(1) What comes first in time is not always the cause of what comes after. Walking is not caused by standing up, and winter does not cause summer. Often both events share one cause: the agent and his choice (προαίρεσις), or the movement of the heavens. So events are not linked "like a chain" (De Fato 25) [passage_alex_fat_25].
(2) Necessity runs backward, not forward. If Socrates exists, Sophroniscus must have been his cause. But Sophroniscus did not have to produce Socrates, just as a foundation does not guarantee a house (De Fato 24) [passage_alex_fat_24; argument_chain_causation_critique_alex].
(3) There are accidental causes, as when someone digging finds treasure. So chance and contingency are real (De Fato 24) [passage_alex_fat_24].
(4) An infinite regress of causes is absurd. There must be first principles (ἀρχή) [passage_alex_fat_25].
(5) The human being is a starting point (ἀρχή) of what he does, with power both to act and not to act. "Nothing without a cause" is kept without giving up what depends on us (τὸ ἐφ' ἡμῖν) (De Fato 12, 20) [28bc2223-dabd-4bb0-933f-faa0fbcc2ef9; d570b896-73be-45f2-bd92-de1ace50cd0b].

Caveat: the KG says Alexander gives no fully worked-out positive theory of causation, and no text proves strong "agent causation" [argument_common_cause_alex; argument_agent_causation_two_way_powers_alexander_q8r9s0t1].
