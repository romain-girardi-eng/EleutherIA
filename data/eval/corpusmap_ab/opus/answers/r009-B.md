ANSWER:
The corpus supports a "Stoic carryover, Christian-Platonist transformation" answer rather than a pure innovation.

Lexically, Frede holds that to autexousion "seems to be of Stoic origin": twice in Musonius, more often in Epictetus, then among Platonists/Peripatetics, and "from Justin Martyr onwards very frequently" in Christian authors [scholarly_argument_frede_autexousion_terminology_4]. Epictetus already defines freedom as being αὐτεξούσιόν τι καὶ αὐτοδέσποτον (Diss. 4.1) [264e75f4-d79b-5483-b5f5-591f912a42de]. Bobzien notes the exousia link in Epictetus "in a deterministic setting" and its rise from the late 2nd century as a replacement for eph' hēmin [scholarly_argument_bobzien_autexousion_as_philosophical_t_6]. Telfer adds a nuance: the adjective αὐτεξούσιος is pre-Christian, but the abstract noun τὸ αὐτεξούσιον emerges only in the second Christian century [scholarly_argument_telfer_development_of_autexousia_conc_2].

What is Christian is the new frame. In this frame God creates angels and humans αὐτεξουσίους (Justin, Dial. 102.4) [7b029b3b-d680-4362-a0ea-8ef4b2aef215]. In Theophilus, God makes man ἐλεύθερον καὶ αὐτεξούσιον (Ad Autol. II.27) [b94d6360-df59-5628-ac3b-3c40f9eb0bbd]. In Gregory of Nyssa, autexousion becomes the imago Dei [argument_gregory_image_dei_autexousion]. Origen's Περὶ αὐτεξουσίου [baa51e4b-9ad7-54a1-a971-74de1631661e] works on "standard Stoic lines" against astral and Gnostic determinism [scholarly_argument_frede_origen_s_doctrine_of_free_will_5]. Sytsma likewise finds the Stoic impression/assent model adopted with alterations [scholarly_argument_sytsma_origen_adopts_stoic_impression_assent]. Frede, however, traces Origen's departures (open alternatives, freedom that cannot be lost) to Platonism, not Christianity [scholarly_argument_frede_origen_s_differences_from_stoi_6]. Fürst calls it an integration of Chrysippean-Epictetan action theory with Platonic alternativity [synthesis_furst2022_origen_central_freedom]. Translating it as "free will" is anachronistic [scholarly_argument_sytsma_autexousion_not_a_faculty_of_will].
