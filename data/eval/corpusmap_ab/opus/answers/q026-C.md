Continuity. Both authors take autexousion as a gift of creation to all rational beings and use it against determinism. Justin says God made "both angels and humans" αὐτεξουσίους for righteous action, while keeping their self-determination intact under his judgments [7b029b3b-d680-4362-a0ea-8ef4b2aef215]. His angel-fall argument places the origin of evil in the misuse of that freedom and treats fate (εἱμαρμένη) as a demonic invention [argument_justin_angel_fall; 0b68baff-e316-4734-a491-acb5acdafee3]. Origen opens De Principiis III.1, "Περὶ αὐτεξουσίου", by appealing to the Church's teaching on God's just judgment and to τὸ ἐφ' ἡμῖν as the basis of praise and blame [baa51e4b-9ad7-54a1-a971-74de1631661e]. That matches Justin's link between freedom and eschatological retribution [synthesis_furst2022_justin_first_christian_freiheits_philosophy]. Hall holds that Origen built on changes Justin started [scholarly_argument_hall_justin_martyr_s_place_in_auton_8]. Frede traces the term from the Stoics (Musonius, Epictetus) into Christian use beginning with Justin [argument_frede_2011_autexousion_stoic_origin_then_christian].

Divergence. In Justin, freedom serves apologetics. He states it repeatedly and proves it from moral change, but he does not analyse it [synthesis_furst2022_justin_first_christian_freiheits_philosophy]. Bobzien notes that Justin does not use the technical exousia framework [scholarly_argument_bobzien_autexousion_as_philosophical_t_6]. Origen sets out to define its ἔννοια and writes the first systematic treatise on freedom rather than on fate [argument_furst_2022_origen_culmination_autexousion]. He grounds it in a theory of self-moved beings [7e1c8576-c630-5099-8e98-082343bca3c6] and explains it "along Stoic lines" inside a Platonist framework [scholarly_argument_frede_origen_s_doctrine_of_free_will_5]. He also changes the opponent. Justin targets Stoic fate and demons. Origen targets Gnostic doctrines of fixed "natures" and astral determinism [scholarly_argument_sytsma_anti_determinism_idle_argument_deprinc_iii; 96064a2b-fbf5-56de-b760-0fd93c9d66b8]. Origen adds that demons keep their freedom permanently and that souls can fall and return [synthesis_frede2011_ch7_origen]. Fürst treats this as Origen developing Justin's first step toward an ontology of freedom [synthesis_furst2022_justin_first_christian_freiheits_philosophy].
