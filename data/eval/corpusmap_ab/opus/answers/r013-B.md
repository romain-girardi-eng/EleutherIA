ANSWER:
The corpus shows clear continuity of argument, but no direct dependence. It holds only the IV.37 passages; IV.38-39 are not attested [work_irenaeus_adversus_haereses_book4].

1. Shared anthropology. In AH IV.37.1, God made man free from the beginning, with his own power (suam potestatem), and placed the power of choice in humans "as in the angels" (quemadmodum et in angelis). God uses no force (Βία Θεῷ οὐ πρόσεστιν) [decb4efc-f086-5663-900a-635c9246b19e; f82703cb-03ab-594d-b492-60c15193a3d0]. This matches Justin: God made angels and humans αὐτεξουσίους πρὸς δικαιοπραξίαν (Dial. 102.4), and they are punished justly because they were created free (2 Apol. 6.5/7.5) [7b029b3b-d680-4362-a0ea-8ef4b2aef215; scholarly_argument_minns_as_justin_s_term_for_free_will_2; argument_justin_angel_fall].

2. Shared anti-determinist topos. IV.37.2 argues that if people were good or bad by nature (φύσει), they would deserve neither praise nor blame. It also has the prophets exhorting "as though it is ἐφ' ἡμῖν" [7c74409a-301c-5e6e-a0a8-ef71a7843cff; a2889e18-9207-5655-8dae-5deffb419d50]. Justin, 1 Apol. 43, uses the same Carneadean praise/blame argument against heimarmene [argument_justin_1apol_43_three_carneadean_topoi; synthesis_amand1945_justin_first_christian_carneadean_user]. Löhr names Justin among the 2nd-century users of this argument [scholarly_argument_l_hr_irenaeus_s_anti_determinist_ar_1].

3. The difference. Justin aims at Stoic or astral fate. Irenaeus turns the argument against the Valentinian doctrine of fixed "natures", with God as judge [argument_irenaeus_adv_haer_iv_37_praise_blame_transposed; argument_irenaeuss_antignostic_argument_for_free_will_f54fe920]. Löhr notes that Irenaeus never compares Valentinian and Stoic determinism directly. IV.37.4 adds a Pauline note: freedom is not to be a cloak for wickedness [4b73889d-a934-57c0-9af1-954c5ba76608].

No source in the corpus says that Irenaeus took this material from Justin. The links rest on shared topoi.
