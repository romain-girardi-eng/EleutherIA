**Continuity.** Autexousion, a term of Stoic origin (Musonius, Epictetus), enters Christian literature with Justin; Origen inherits it [argument_frede_2011_autexousion_stoic_origin_then_christian; scholarly_argument_frede_autexousion_terminology_4]. Hall sees Origen building on changes Justin started [scholarly_argument_hall_justin_martyr_s_place_in_auton_8]. Both tie self-determination to God's just judgment. Justin says God made angels and humans "αὐτεξουσίους πρὸς δικαιοπραξίαν" and judges them "πεφυλαγμένου μέντοι τοῦ αὐτεξουσίου" [7b029b3b-d680-4362-a0ea-8ef4b2aef215]. In 2 Apol. 6(7).5, free will grounds eternal retribution, against Stoic fate [scholarly_argument_minns_as_justin_s_term_for_free_will_2; argument_justin_angel_fall]. Origen starts his Περὶ αὐτεξουσίου from the church teaching on God's just judgment and from τὸ ἐφ' ἡμῖν [baa51e4b-9ad7-54a1-a971-74de1631661e]. **Divergence.** For Justin, autexousion is an apologetic axiom, used in a few passages (1 Apol. 43-44; 2 Apol. 5-7; Dial. 88, 102, 141) to answer fate [argument_justin_martyrs_apologetic_argument_for_free_will_45c2dde2]. Justin draws on Carneadean and Middle Platonist material [synthesis_amand1945_justin_first_christian_carneadean_user; argument_justin_equals_alcinous_heimarmene]. Sin and grace play no role in his theology [scholarly_argument_andresen_sin_and_grace_in_justin_s_theo_3]. Origen makes the term a central technical concept: he asks for its ἔννοια and writes the first systematic treatise "On Freedom" [argument_furst_2022_origen_culmination_autexousion; synthesis_frede2011_ch7_origen]. Frede finds Origen's content largely Epictetan Stoic, aimed at Gnostics and astral determinism. Origen's departures from the Stoics come from his Platonism: he equates autexousion with eph' hēmin, and he holds that freedom is never lost, which allows fall and return (apokatastasis) [scholarly_argument_frede_origen_s_doctrine_of_free_will_5; synthesis_frede2011_ch7_origen].
