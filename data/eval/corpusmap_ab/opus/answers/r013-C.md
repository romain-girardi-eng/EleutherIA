ANSWER:
The KG presents Adv. Haer. IV.37-39 as Irenaeus's locus classicus on αὐτεξουσία against Valentinian determinism, and says he "builds directly on" Justin (1 Apol. 43-44; Dial. 141), turning Justin's position into a systematic developmental framework [work_irenaeus_adversus_haereses_book4]. The texts support this continuity on three points.

(1) The praise/blame topos. IV.37.2 argues that if people were good or bad by nature, they would deserve neither praise nor blame. All share one nature and can keep or reject the good, and the prophets exhort "as though it depends on us" (ὡς ἐφ' ἡμῖν) [7c74409a-301c-5e6e-a0a8-ef71a7843cff; a2889e18-9207-5655-8dae-5deffb419d50]. Justin's 1 Apol. 43 has the same Carneadean argument "in skeletal form" [argument_justin_1apol_43_three_carneadean_topoi]. Löhr says Justin also used this argument [scholarly_argument_l_hr_irenaeus_s_anti_determinist_ar_1]. Amand reads Irenaeus as a distant, Christianized echo: virtue becomes obedience, and the rewards become eternal [argument_irenaeus_adv_haer_iv_37_praise_blame_transposed; synthesis_amand1945_irenaeus_transposed_topos].

(2) Angels and humans. IV.37.1 says God placed "potestatem electionis" in man, as in the angels, and does not use force [decb4efc-f086-5663-900a-635c9246b19e; f82703cb-03ab-594d-b492-60c15193a3d0]. This parallels Dial. 88.5, where God makes angels and humans αὐτεξούσιοι [ad0049c4-8fa3-417c-9f02-42a98b0a862f].

(3) Freedom from compulsion. IV.37.4 stresses τὸ ἐλεύθερον, with God not compelling anyone [4b73889d-a934-57c0-9af1-954c5ba76608].

Differences: the opponent changes from Stoic/astral fate to Gnostic natures [argument_irenaeuss_antignostic_argument_for_free_will_f54fe920], and the scriptural proof takes the lead. The νήπιος/τέλειος growth doctrine of IV.38-39 is described as Irenaeus's own innovation [work_irenaeus_adversus_haereses_book4]. The corpus holds no passages from IV.38-39, so those chapters are not directly attested. Löhr also notes that Irenaeus never explicitly equates Valentinian and Stoic determinism [scholarly_argument_l_hr_irenaeus_s_anti_determinist_ar_1].
