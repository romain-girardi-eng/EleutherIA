ANSWER:
The EleutherIA corpus does not attest the content of Tertullian, De anima 21, nor any of J. H. Waszink's commentary on it. Its evidence is limited to the following.

1. The work exists in the KG as Tertullian, De Anima (c. 210–213 CE). The node cites Waszink's edition (CCSL 2) and describes the work as a 58-chapter treatise, the earliest systematic Latin work on the soul, that engages extensively with Stoic, Platonic and medical sources [work_tertullian_de_anima]. The node also states that no passages of De anima are in the database: the 31 passages once attributed to it are from Adversus Praxean [work_tertullian_de_anima; work_tertullian_adversus_praxean]. Its only graph link is authorship [work_tertullian_de_anima → person_tertullian_d220].

2. The Tertullian node says De anima develops a traducian theory of the soul, with consequences for inherited moral corruption and the scope of responsibility. Tertullian's explicit defence of liberum arbitrium sits in Adversus Marcionem II.5–7, against Marcion's dualism, not in an anti-Stoic text [person_tertullian_d220; argument_tertullians_antimarcionite_argument_for_free_will_f49cad73].

3. Préaux holds that Tertullian and Cyprian are "strangely silent" on fate and divine providence, and that no complete Latin treatment exists before Augustine [scholarly_argument_pr_aux_tertullian_and_cyprian_on_fate_3]. This weighs against reading Tertullian as a systematic anti-determinist.

Searches of the passage corpus found no text of De anima 21 and no Waszink commentary. Any specific claim about chapter 21's argument against Stoic determinism (for example, the soul's changeable nature and free choice) would therefore be unsupported here.
