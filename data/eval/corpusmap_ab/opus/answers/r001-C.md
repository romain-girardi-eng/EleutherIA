Text. In 1 Apol. 43 Justin argues by reductio: if everything happens "by fate" (καθ' εἱμαρμένην), "what is up to us" (τὸ ἐφ' ἡμῖν) does not exist at all, and praise and blame lose their point. Humans act by "free choice" (προαιρέσει ἐλευθέρᾳ) [c2b1124e-da5c-55e6-9549-79033bc71a2f; argument_justin_antifatalism]. In 2 Apol. 7 (6.5 in Minns–Parvis), God made angels and humans αὐτεξούσιον, which answers the Stoic claim that all comes about by the necessity of fate. Fate itself becomes a demonic deception [scholarly_argument_minns_as_justin_s_term_for_free_will_2; argument_justin_angel_fall; 0b68baff-e316-4734-a491-acb5acdafee3].

Andresen (continuity). Justin is a Middle Platonist of the "orthodox" Plutarch/Atticus line [scholarly_argument_andresen_justin_s_philosophical_classif_0]. His anti-Stoic arguments are "weithin identisch" ("largely identical") with Alcinous, Didask. 26. Both hold that the soul is free in its choices, while the consequences of those choices fall under heimarmene [scholarly_argument_andresen_heimarmene_fate_and_free_will_1; argument_justin_equals_alcinous_heimarmene]. The only difference Andresen notes is that Justin argues ethically and Alcinous metaphysically [scholarly_argument_andresen_methodological_distinction_met_4]. Boys-Stones also counts Justin as a witness to hypothetical fate [scholarly_argument_boys_stones_justin_martyr_as_middle_platon_7].

Karamanolis (a different lineage). The KG has no Justin-specific claim from Karamanolis. In general he holds that τὸ ἐφ' ἡμῖν, προαίρεσις and αὐτεξούσιον are Stoic in origin. He says Christians took up the Stoic notion because they shared Stoic theological assumptions, and he reads them as a distinct school [scholarly_argument_karamanolis_free_will_and_divine_providenc_0; scholarly_argument_karamanolis_status_of_early_christian_phil_1]. On his view the term in 2 Apol. 7 points away from Middle Platonism. Bobzien adds that αὐτεξούσιον becomes common only from the late 2nd century onward [scholarly_argument_bobzien_autexousion_as_philosophical_t_6].

Conclusion. The argument structure of 1 Apol. 43 is continuous with Middle Platonism (Andresen). The vocabulary and the creationist, demonological setting of 2 Apol. 7 are not. The corpus does not show Karamanolis replying to Andresen directly.
