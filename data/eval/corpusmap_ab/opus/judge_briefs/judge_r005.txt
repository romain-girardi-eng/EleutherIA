QUESTION: Methodius's anti-Origenist De Libero Arbitrio: genuine controversy or rhetorical exercise?
Reference claims a good answer should support:
- Methodius holds that the first human was made autexousion (self-determining), not made evil by God.
- Methodius contrasts human freedom with the natural elements (heaven, sun, earth) that serve God's command by necessity (ananke), unable to do otherwise.
- Methodius argues that humans alone have the power (exousia) to choose whom to obey, which is why obedience by the necessitated elements earns no praise.

--- ANSWER X ---
ANSWER:
The corpus does not support calling De Libero Arbitrio (De Autexusio) itself anti-Origenist. Methodius is described as Origen's principal adversary and organiser of the anti-Origenist reaction (Amand 1945), but the theses he attacked there are creation ab aeterno, pre-existence and fall of souls, and a spiritualised resurrection [synthesis_amand1945_methodius_anti_origenist_reaction; person_methodius_olympus_d311]. The target in De Autexusio is a different one: a speaker puts forward the thesis that God shaped pre-existent, formless, disorderly matter (ὕλη) and that evil flows from its unworked remainder [420bc218-e4f5-42cd-ad66-b4199d146805; passage_meth_dla_16]. The KG classes this as a Gnostic/Middle-Platonist view and the treatise as anti-Gnostic [argument_methodius_theodicy_autexousion]. Methodius rebuts it dialectically (composite matter would have come into being, so it cannot be ungenerated; God would be "contained" by matter) [4391cb68-8928-4434-ba07-33839367b7fd; b54894a6-8fa0-43f0-b229-f21bb17e5e64]. He then places evil in disobedience by a self-determining human being [cee5e3d9-5b28-445f-bf5c-ed78c98ca633; f5357af8-2b56-48a4-abc2-8a2e86720416].

The doctrine reads as real and pointed rather than a mere exercise. Free will as a gift of obedience is an Irenaean theme, and the same thesis recurs in the Symposium [argument_methodius_libre_arbitre_obeissance_irenean]. There is a scholastic side as well. The work is a staged dialogue with speaker labels [f5357af8-2b56-48a4-abc2-8a2e86720416]. Amand also argues that Methodius took his anti-fatalist syllogisms (Symposium VIII.16) almost word for word from a school hypomnema. Amand says the De Autex. 16 echo is more probably Irenaean [synthesis_amand1945_methodius_indirect_via_hypomnema].

Verdict: the corpus shows a real anti-dualist polemic in a scholastic dialogue form. Nothing in the corpus documents a specifically anti-Origenist aim for this treatise.

ANSWER:
First, the premise needs correcting. The KG does not describe De Libero Arbitrio (De autexusio) as anti-Origenist. Its stated targets are Gnostic and Valentinian views that evil comes from pre-existent matter (ὕλη) or from God [work_methodius_de_libero_arbitrio; work_methodius_de_autexusio_4c37c892; argument_methodius_theodicy_autexousion]. Methodius's anti-Origenist polemic (against pre-existence of souls and endless succession of worlds) is attached to De resurrectione [work_methodius_de_resurrectione_bb76b99d; person_methodius_olympus_d311].

On "controversy or exercise", the corpus points both ways, and no scholarly node settles it:
- Literary-exercise markers: the work follows Platonic dialogue conventions [work_methodius_de_libero_arbitrio]. It opens with a scenic seaside walk ("Χθὲς τὸ δειλινὸν περιπατῶν… παρὰ τὸν τῆς θαλάσσης αἰγιαλόν") [dcebc4ae-f52e-4b60-b823-c5ba9eb8bd28]. The speakers explicitly frame the inquiry as friendly, not contentious: "εἰ μὲν ἐκ φιλονεικίας… ἐπεὶ δὲ φιλίας ἕνεκα… τὴν ἐξέτασιν ποιούμεθα" [e8e8c962-561f-4bd2-9fb5-516f91587ff4]. The opponent is only "possibly Valentinus" [work_methodius_de_libero_arbitrio].
- Real doctrinal stakes: the dialectic against uncreated matter is detailed and technical [b54894a6-8fa0-43f0-b229-f21bb17e5e64; 4391cb68-8928-4434-ba07-33839367b7fd]. The positive thesis, that the first human was created αὐτεξούσιον and evil is disobedience, is argued in earnest [f5357af8-2b56-48a4-abc2-8a2e86720416; cee5e3d9-5b28-445f-bf5c-ed78c98ca633].

Verdict: the work is best read as a genuine doctrinal polemic against a real position (Gnostic/Valentinian dualism of matter) set in a stylised Platonic dialogue form. Whether there was a historical exchange with an actual opponent, or any veiled anti-Origenist aim, is not attested in the corpus.

ANSWER:
The corpus supports a qualified answer: a real doctrinal controversy, conducted in a literary-scholastic form.

1. Target. Within the corpus, De Autexusio is not aimed at Origen. Its opponent holds that God made the world from pre-existent, formless, disorderly matter (ὕλη), and that evil flows from the leftover parts of that matter [passage_meth_dla_16; 420bc218-e4f5-42cd-ad66-b4199d146805]. The KG classes this view as Gnostic or Middle Platonist [argument_methodius_theodicy_autexousion]. The anti-Origenist label comes from Amand's verdict on Methodius as a whole: he organized the church's reaction against creation ab aeterno, the pre-existence of souls and a spiritualized resurrection, while sharing Origen's Platonism [synthesis_amand1945_methodius_anti_origenist_reaction; person_methodius_olympus_d311]. Nothing returned ties De Autexusio itself to Origen's theses.

2. Signs of an exercise. The work is a staged dialogue with speaker labels ΟΡΘΟΔ. and ΟΥΑΔ. Its speaker says the inquiry is held "for friendship and the neighbour's benefit", not "from contentiousness" (φιλονεικία) [e8e8c962-561f-4bd2-9fb5-516f91587ff4]. A hymnic prologue addresses an assembly of listeners [5baf8977-eb6e-4ae0-bb1b-3837381d8741]. Amand finds Methodius drawing indirectly on school handbooks (hypomnemata), copied almost word for word. He shows this for the Symposium, and De Autex. 16 has only a faint echo that is more probably Irenaean [synthesis_amand1945_methodius_indirect_via_hypomnema; argument_methodius_libre_arbitre_obeissance_irenean].

3. Signs of real doctrinal stakes. The argument is a sustained theodicy. Evil comes neither from matter nor from God but from humans misusing their self-determination (αὐτεξούσιον) [argument_methodius_theodicy_autexousion; f5357af8-2b56-48a4-abc2-8a2e86720416]. Freedom is God's greatest gift: the power to obey without constraint [argument_methodius_libre_arbitre_obeissance_irenean].

The corpus has no explicit scholarly discussion of "controversy versus exercise".
