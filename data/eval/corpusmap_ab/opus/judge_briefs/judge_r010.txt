ANSWER:
The Treatise of the Two Spirits (1QS III.13–IV.26) is treated as the most explicit predestinarian text of Second Temple Judaism: "Before they existed He established their whole design" (III.15); God creates a Spirit of Truth and a Spirit of Injustice and assigns each person to one "according to their inheritance", by birth and not by merit. Behind them stand the Prince of Light and the Angel of Darkness, until the "appointed end" [argument_qumran_predestination_c3d4e5f6; concept_two_spirits_doctrine_s7t8u9v0]. Klawans reads this as the core of the Essene determinism that Josephus reports (Ant. 13.172) [scholarly_argument_klawans_2012_essene_determinism_matches_qumran]. Scholars disagree about how strict it is. Lange sees double predestination close to Calvinism. Frey sees a modified dualism that leaves room for human decision [argument_qumran_predestination_c3d4e5f6]. Brand reads the Treatise as combining predestination with an inner struggle between the two spirits [scholarly_argument_brand_two_spirits_synthesizes_views]. She also finds a free-choice redaction in the 1QS Rule [scholarly_argument_brand_covenantal_texts_free_choice] and argues that a text's genre, not a single "Qumran theology", sets its position [scholarly_argument_brand_genre_determines_free_will_view].

On the Patristic side, Augustine taught single predestination of the elect, with the reprobate passed over (praeteritio), grounded in Romans 9 [concept_predestination_augustinian]. This came out of the debate with Pelagius (411–431), and the Council of Orange (529) later moderated its extreme form [debate_augustine_pelagius_grace]. The only links are analogies drawn by scholars: Lange's Calvinist comparison, and Eskola's warning that Calvin's determinism has coloured readings of Second Temple Judaism [scholarly_argument_eskola_double_predestination_3].
