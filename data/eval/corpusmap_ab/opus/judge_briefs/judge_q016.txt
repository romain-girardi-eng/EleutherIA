QUESTION: Christian apologists against Gnostic fatalism
Reference claims a good answer should support:
- Justin denies that events happen by fated necessity (kath' heimarmenēn anankēn) merely because God foreknows them.
- Justin argues that if all things happened by fate there would be nothing 'up to us' (to eph' hēmin), and neither the good person would be praiseworthy nor the bad blameworthy.

ANSWER:
The KG presents the Christian–Gnostic dispute as a patristic debate opposing human self-determination (autexousion) to cosmic and "natural" determinism [debate_christian_gnostic_freedom; concept_autexousion_christian_freedom_u1v2w3x4]. Fürst argues that the fight against Marcionite, Valentinian and Basilidean predestinarian teaching drove the apologists (Justin, Theophilus, Tatian, Irenaeus, Clement, Tertullian) to make freedom a central polemical issue [argument_furst_2022_irenaeus_against_gnostic_natures].

- Justin: foreknowledge is not causation. Reward and punishment "according to merit" would make no sense if everything happened by heimarmenē. God made angels and humans αὐτεξουσίους [argument_justin_martyrs_apologetic_argument_for_free_will_45c2dde2; 9a8ffeae-8eab-445f-9475-5071fc3590f4].
- Tatian refuses a birth "according to fate" (γένεσιν τὴν καθ᾿ εἱμαρμένην). This is aimed at astral fate, not explicitly at Gnostics [f8ceab87-f393-4ca6-aceb-e28dd1346abe].
- The Greek apologists hold that God created rational beings free, so evil comes from creatures' choice [scholarly_argument_pouderon_free_will_libre_arbitre_in_gre_0].
- Irenaeus, against fixed pneumatic/psychic/hylic natures, argues by reductio (Adv. Haer. IV 37,6): if the good were good "by nature" rather than by will, their goodness would be nothing of their own [argument_irenaeuss_antignostic_argument_for_free_will_f54fe920; argument_furst_2022_irenaeus_against_gnostic_natures].
- Tertullian answers Marcion's dualism with a free-will theodicy [argument_tertullians_antimarcionite_argument_for_free_will_f49cad73].
- Origen attacks "heterodox" who bring in natures that are saved or lost by constitution. He uses Pharaoh's hardening against them [6c5f98e0-6642-53e7-8b27-5cdadb9b4cf2; 176cd86a-4580-4277-a919-5d01d827b3dd; scholarly_argument_sytsma_anti_determinism_idle_argument_deprinc_iii].

ANSWER:
The knowledge graph models this topic as the "Christian-Gnostic Debate on Freedom": a patristic dispute over human freedom (autexousion) against cosmic determinism [debate_christian_gnostic_freedom; concept_autexousion_christian_freedom_u1v2w3x4]. Its positions are fatalism, theological determinism and libertarian free will [position_fatalism; position_theological_determinism; position_libertarianism_freewill]. Origen is the named Christian participant, and De Principiis is the source text [person_origen_alexandria_185_254ce_s9t0u1v2; work_de_principiis_origen_230s_v2w3x4y5]. Junod reads that treatise as a rigorous rational answer to Christians who were drawn to Gnostic claims to knowledge [scholarly_argument_junod_origenian_theology_answers_gnostic_intellectual_demand].

From c. 150, the Apologists used the vocabulary of choice in a quasi-technical way. They affirmed the "initial freedom" that the Creator gave to every creature (Harl) [scholarly_argument_harl_christian_choice; school_christian_apologists]. Justin, for example, describes the true Israel as those who choose (αἱρουμένους) to be persuaded through Christ [a0fb6da6-bfbd-49a2-a79a-22c0d81053fc]. Melito says Adam was "by nature capable of receiving good and evil" [7ba4eaeb-14e5-448b-9de1-5b60bd2134e7]. Pouderon's book says the Apologists opposed the Gnostics, but its back cover is about the Demiurge, Christology and the resurrection. It makes no claim about fate [scholarly_argument_pouderon_determinism_and_fate_vs_christ_1].

Scholars disagree about whether the target existed:
- Telfer: Basilides and Valentinus rejected autexousia, so fixed natures decide salvation [position_telfer_valentinian_natural_determinism].
- Linjamaa: the Tripartite Tractate keeps self-determination and accountability [position_linjamaa_valentinian_ethics_and_self_determination].
- Löhr: "Gnostic determinism" is a heresiological cliché, begun by Irenaeus, developed by Clement and perfected by Origen [scholarly_argument_l_hr_gnostic_determinism_as_heresio_0].

The tools returned no anti-Gnostic passage from Irenaeus, Clement or Origen on fate [person_irenaeus_d202].
