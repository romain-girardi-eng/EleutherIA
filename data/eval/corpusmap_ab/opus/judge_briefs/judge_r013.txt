QUESTION: Irenaeus Adversus Haereses IV.37-39 on free will: continuity with Justin Martyr?

--- ANSWER X ---
ANSWER:
The corpus shows clear continuity of argument, but no direct dependence. It holds only the IV.37 passages; IV.38-39 are not attested [work_irenaeus_adversus_haereses_book4].

1. Shared anthropology. In AH IV.37.1, God made man free from the beginning, with his own power (suam potestatem), and placed the power of choice in humans "as in the angels" (quemadmodum et in angelis). God uses no force (Βία Θεῷ οὐ πρόσεστιν) [decb4efc-f086-5663-900a-635c9246b19e; f82703cb-03ab-594d-b492-60c15193a3d0]. This matches Justin: God made angels and humans αὐτεξουσίους πρὸς δικαιοπραξίαν (Dial. 102.4), and they are punished justly because they were created free (2 Apol. 6.5/7.5) [7b029b3b-d680-4362-a0ea-8ef4b2aef215; scholarly_argument_minns_as_justin_s_term_for_free_will_2; argument_justin_angel_fall].

2. Shared anti-determinist topos. IV.37.2 argues that if people were good or bad by nature (φύσει), they would deserve neither praise nor blame. It also has the prophets exhorting "as though it is ἐφ' ἡμῖν" [7c74409a-301c-5e6e-a0a8-ef71a7843cff; a2889e18-9207-5655-8dae-5deffb419d50]. Justin, 1 Apol. 43, uses the same Carneadean praise/blame argument against heimarmene [argument_justin_1apol_43_three_carneadean_topoi; synthesis_amand1945_justin_first_christian_carneadean_user]. Löhr names Justin among the 2nd-century users of this argument [scholarly_argument_l_hr_irenaeus_s_anti_determinist_ar_1].

3. The difference. Justin aims at Stoic or astral fate. Irenaeus turns the argument against the Valentinian doctrine of fixed "natures", with God as judge [argument_irenaeus_adv_haer_iv_37_praise_blame_transposed; argument_irenaeuss_antignostic_argument_for_free_will_f54fe920]. Löhr notes that Irenaeus never compares Valentinian and Stoic determinism directly. IV.37.4 adds a Pauline note: freedom is not to be a cloak for wickedness [4b73889d-a934-57c0-9af1-954c5ba76608].

No source in the corpus says that Irenaeus took this material from Justin. The links rest on shared topoi.

ANSWER:
The KG presents Adv. Haer. IV.37-39 as Irenaeus's locus classicus on αὐτεξουσία against Valentinian determinism, and says he "builds directly on" Justin (1 Apol. 43-44; Dial. 141), turning Justin's position into a systematic developmental framework [work_irenaeus_adversus_haereses_book4]. The texts support this continuity on three points.

(1) The praise/blame topos. IV.37.2 argues that if people were good or bad by nature, they would deserve neither praise nor blame. All share one nature and can keep or reject the good, and the prophets exhort "as though it depends on us" (ὡς ἐφ' ἡμῖν) [7c74409a-301c-5e6e-a0a8-ef71a7843cff; a2889e18-9207-5655-8dae-5deffb419d50]. Justin's 1 Apol. 43 has the same Carneadean argument "in skeletal form" [argument_justin_1apol_43_three_carneadean_topoi]. Löhr says Justin also used this argument [scholarly_argument_l_hr_irenaeus_s_anti_determinist_ar_1]. Amand reads Irenaeus as a distant, Christianized echo: virtue becomes obedience, and the rewards become eternal [argument_irenaeus_adv_haer_iv_37_praise_blame_transposed; synthesis_amand1945_irenaeus_transposed_topos].

(2) Angels and humans. IV.37.1 says God placed "potestatem electionis" in man, as in the angels, and does not use force [decb4efc-f086-5663-900a-635c9246b19e; f82703cb-03ab-594d-b492-60c15193a3d0]. This parallels Dial. 88.5, where God makes angels and humans αὐτεξούσιοι [ad0049c4-8fa3-417c-9f02-42a98b0a862f].

(3) Freedom from compulsion. IV.37.4 stresses τὸ ἐλεύθερον, with God not compelling anyone [4b73889d-a934-57c0-9af1-954c5ba76608].

Differences: the opponent changes from Stoic/astral fate to Gnostic natures [argument_irenaeuss_antignostic_argument_for_free_will_f54fe920], and the scriptural proof takes the lead. The νήπιος/τέλειος growth doctrine of IV.38-39 is described as Irenaeus's own innovation [work_irenaeus_adversus_haereses_book4]. The corpus holds no passages from IV.38-39, so those chapters are not directly attested. Löhr also notes that Irenaeus never explicitly equates Valentinian and Stoic determinism [scholarly_argument_l_hr_irenaeus_s_anti_determinist_ar_1].

ANSWER:
The KG records Justin as influencing Irenaeus [person_justin_martyr_2c_ce; person_irenaeus_d202], and describes AH IV.37-39 as building on Justin's αὐτεξούσιον (1 Apol. 43-44; Dial. 141) [work_irenaeus_adversus_haereses_book4].

Continuity, visible in the texts:
(1) Both authors say God created angels and humans alike as self-determining. Justin: God "ἐποίησεν αὐτεξουσίους πρὸς δικαιοπραξίαν καὶ ἀγγέλους καὶ ἀνθρώπους" [7b029b3b-d680-4362-a0ea-8ef4b2aef215; 9a8ffeae-8eab-445f-9475-5071fc3590f4; ad0049c4-8fa3-417c-9f02-42a98b0a862f]. Irenaeus: "posuit in homine potestatem electionis, quemadmodum et in angelis" [decb4efc-f086-5663-900a-635c9246b19e].
(2) Both say God does not coerce. Irenaeus has "Βία Θεῷ οὐ πρόσεστιν" [f82703cb-03ab-594d-b492-60c15193a3d0] and "μὴ καταναγκάζοντος αὐτὸν τοῦ Θεοῦ" [4b73889d-a934-57c0-9af1-954c5ba76608].
(3) Both use ἐφ' ἡμῖν and tie praise, blame and reward or punishment to that power [argument_justin_antifatalism; 7c74409a-301c-5e6e-a0a8-ef71a7843cff; a2889e18-9207-5655-8dae-5deffb419d50].

Shifts:
- Different opponent. Justin targets Stoic εἱμαρμένη [argument_justin_martyrs_apologetic_argument_for_free_will_45c2dde2]. Irenaeus targets the Valentinian idea of fixed natures: "εἰ φύσει οἱ μὲν φαῦλοι…" [7c74409a-301c-5e6e-a0a8-ef71a7843cff; argument_irenaeuss_antignostic_argument_for_free_will_f54fe920]. The KG reads this as an echo of Carneades' argument, adapted to Christian use [argument_irenaeus_adv_haer_iv_37_praise_blame_transposed].
- A developmental anthropology. The KG credits the νήπιος doctrine (IV.38) to Theophilus first, not to Justin [concept_nepios_adam_infant_doctrine]. Its "good vs. perfect" framework and pedagogical theodicy are Irenaeus's own extension [concept_agathos_vs_teleios_distinction; concept_pedagogical_theodicy].

Limits: the corpus contains passages only for IV.37.1-4. I found no text from IV.38-39 (apart from the IV.38.1 fragment quoted inside the concept node) and no explicit citation of Justin by Irenaeus. The continuity rests on parallel wording and the KG's influence edge.
