Qumran side. The Treatise of the Two Spirits (1QS III.13–IV.26) is described as the most sustained predestinarian text of Second Temple Judaism: "From the God of Knowledge comes all that is and shall be" (1QS III.15); God made the Spirit of Truth and the Spirit of Injustice and assigned each person to one at creation (III.18–19); the Angel of Darkness misleads even the sons of light (III.22–24), which raises the question of responsibility [argument_qumran_predestination_c3d4e5f6; concept_two_spirits_doctrine_s7t8u9v0]. Scholars disagree: Lange reads strict double predestination, Frey a modified dualism preserving human decision [argument_qumran_predestination_c3d4e5f6]. Brand reads the Treatise as merging internal/external and free-will/predestinarian views [scholarly_argument_brand_two_spirits_synthesizes_views]; she argues that a text's view of free will correlates with its genre, so there is no single "Qumran theology" [scholarly_argument_brand_genre_determines_free_will_view]; and she finds covenantal texts (CD, 1QS) stressing free choice [scholarly_argument_brand_covenantal_texts_free_choice]. Klawans equates Josephus's Essene "fate" with Qumran predestinarianism [scholarly_argument_klawans_2012_essene_determinism_matches_qumran].

Patristic side. Augustinian predestination holds that God elects some and passes over others, that grace is irresistible for the elect, and that post-Fall free will can only sin. It is grounded in Romans 9 and anti-Pelagian argument, and Semi-Pelagians contested it [concept_predestination_augustinian].
