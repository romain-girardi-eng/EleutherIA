The KG models Strom. II.6-15 as a "grace-freedom synergy via Stoic assent" [argument_clement_grace_synergy_assent]. Clement borrows the Stoic theory of προαίρεσις and συγκατάθεσις: impressions do not compel, and assent is the agent's own. He Christianizes it by making faith an act of free assent, "a voluntary assent" (the node cites II.2.8 and V.13.86 for this). Grace draws the soul but does not compel it, so assent is the soul's own and also a gift of the Logos [argument_clement_grace_synergy_assent]. The node's description is a KG summary; I did not read the passages behind it in full.

The corpus attests the vocabulary of responsibility in II.15. Voluntariness is divided into appetite, choice or intellect, and our obedience to the commandments is "up to us" (ἐφ' ἡμῖν) [15512370-1bd6-56f3-a8c2-d88ad954af3e, II.15.62]. Clement also grades wrongs. Ἀτύχημα is an unforeseen error. Ἁμαρτία is involuntary injustice, while ἀδικία that is voluntary is vice (κακία). Only ἀδικία is wholly voluntary [d9c85f6f-4445-5c3a-8560-c75327f999c3, II.15.64]. The Stoic-Socratic claim that error is involuntary appears in Epictetus [d870c668-956a-5fb0-81b4-61f03a62e102, Diss. 1.28].

The Christian additions are repentance and forgiveness. Baptism remits earlier sins committed in ignorance. Post-baptismal sin calls for repentance and shame, and Clement warns of the devil's accusations [b58f180c-43c6-521e-9639-42249a57672b, II.13.56; 1b692a47-cbfa-5307-b120-e3ee3a412ae8, II.13.58]. Sins are covered or blotted out by God, who prefers the sinner's repentance to his death [d9c85f6f-4445-5c3a-8560-c75327f999c3, II.15.65]. Fear of the Lord is also reinterpreted as a non-passionate prudence [d55384c9-bf4e-5b19-910f-fc362155f143, II.8.40].

The corpus returned no passage showing Clement explicitly naming or criticizing the Stoics in these chapters. The Stoic/Christian contrast above is therefore inference from the KG node.
