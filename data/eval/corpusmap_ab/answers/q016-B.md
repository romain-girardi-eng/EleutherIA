The KG presents the Christian-Gnostic debate as a patristic contest between autexousion and cosmic determinism [debate_christian_gnostic_freedom]. Irenaeus wrote against Valentinian and other systems that divided humanity into predetermined types (pneumatikoi, psychikoi, hylikoi), arguing that such determinism destroys genuine humanity and morality; the node calls it one of the first systematic Christian theologies of freedom [argument_irenaeuss_antignostic_argument_for_free_will_f54fe920]. Justin Martyr rebuts the charges that Christian prophecy entails fatalism and that Christian cosmology shares Stoic heimarmene or Gnostic necessity. He distinguishes foreknowledge from causation (1 Apol. 43), invokes to eph' hemin, and holds that evil comes from free choice, not from matter (2 Apol. 5-7; Dial. 102, 141) [argument_justin_martyrs_apologetic_argument_for_free_will_45c2dde2]. Pouderon reads the Greek Apologists (Justin, Tatian, Theophilus, Athenagoras) as affirming that God made humans and angels free (libre arbitre), so evil follows from that gift [scholarly_argument_pouderon_free_will_libre_arbitre_in_gre_0]. Tertullian answers Marcion's dualism through a free-will theodicy [argument_tertullians_antimarcionite_argument_for_free_will_f49cad73]. Origen attacks Gnostic fixed "natures" as destroying prohairesis and autexousion, culminating in De Principiis III.1 [scholarly_argument_sytsma_anti_determinism_idle_argument_deprinc_iii]. Caveat: Löhr treats the charge of Gnostic determinism as a heresiological construct "inaugurated by Irenaeus, developed by Clement, perfected by Origen". He leaves open whether it fairly describes any Gnostic source [scholarly_argument_l_hr_gnostic_determinism_as_heresio_0]. Note that Justin and Tertullian are only partly "apologists against Gnostics" (Tertullian's target is Marcion).
