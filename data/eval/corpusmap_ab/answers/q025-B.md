Aristotle: what is "up to us" (eph' hêmin) is tied to voluntary action with an internal origin (archê) and knowledge [evolution_eph_hemin_6284cf6d]. It is two-sided: "where to act is up to us, also not to act is up to us" (EN III.5, 1113b7-8) [1e336c5e-391c-5613-a59d-b83d2bcc5523]. It grounds character responsibility (EN III.5, 1114b1-12) [28b16b62-34cb-4db0-a445-c778d696cb4e], and rational powers are powers of opposites (Metaph. IX) [argument_two_way_powers_aristotle_i9j0k1l2]. Prohairesis is defined as deliberative desire of things that depend on us [scholarly_argument_crubellier_two_definitions_of_prohairesis_1]. Bobzien holds that this does not entail indeterminism [scholarly_argument_bobzien_aristotle_s_conception_of_2].

Stoics: Bobzien distinguishes two early Stoic notions. "What happens through us" is one-sided and causative, a necessary causal factor that Chrysippus shows to be compatible with fate. "What depends on us" is two-sided and potestative, neutral on determinism, and a necessary condition of responsibility; the sources rule out an indeterminist reading [scholarly_argument_bobzien_to_eph_hemin_what_depends_on_u_4; argument_bobzien_2001_b1_eph_hemin_one_vs_two_sided]. The conceptual-evolution note says the Stoics redefine it as assent (synkatathesis) to impressions [evolution_eph_hemin_6284cf6d]. Epictetus makes it the basis of ethics, confining it primarily to the use of impressions and to prohairesis [scholarly_argument_bobzien_epictetus_on_freedom_and_what__3; dd5cfa96-1255-5ae1-b912-8e3216a288bc]. Gourinat sees Epictetus borrowing prohairesis from Aristotelianism and transforming it [scholarly_argument_gourinat_prohairesis_as_origin_and_tran_0].

Comparison: both are read as compatible with determinism by Bobzien; Aristotle's scope is wider (actions, character), the Stoic/Epictetan scope narrower and internal. Frede argues the "free will" resonance appears only with Alexander, Epictetus and Christians [argument_frede_michael_2014_eph_hemin_emerges_with_alexander_epictetus_christianity]. Note that the Aristotle readings are contested (Eliasson, Echeñique), which I did not examine further.
