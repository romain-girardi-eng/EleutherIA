The Academic attack is chiefly Carneades' (head of the New Academy) against Chrysippus and Antipater, preserved mainly in Cicero, De Fato 28-44 [debate_stoic_academic_hellenistic]. According to Amand, the target was the Stoic joint defence of absolute fatalism (heimarmene) and of Babylonian divination and astrology. Carneades ran two lines: a technical critique of astrology, and an indirect moral critique of fatalism's consequences [debate_stoic_academic_hellenistic].

1. The core dilemma (De Fato 31). If everything happens by antecedent causes closely bound by natural connection, necessity governs all and nothing is in our power. But some things are in our power. Therefore not everything happens by fate [504a4992-9bbe-5652-a96c-530ad613667b; 9c90be4e-5aff-5408-835b-9bc44724f0c1]. Cicero presents Carneades as pressing this argument without endorsing a position himself [9c90be4e-5aff-5408-835b-9bc44724f0c1].

2. Voluntary movement. Carneades accepted "nothing without a cause" but held that a voluntary movement's cause lies in the will itself. He distinguished necessary external causes from contingent causes grounded in the subject. This rejects both Chrysippean determinism and the Epicurean swerve [argument_carneades_autonomous_mental_causation_argument_4e7e9250; d73d4ce4-4f12-5340-a38c-22e3d6745a91].

3. Chrysippus' cylinder distinction. Amand reads Carneades as showing that the distinction between perfect/principal and auxiliary/proximate causes is merely verbal. On this reading it confirms the sovereignty of fate [argument_carneades_autonomous_mental_causation_argument_4e7e9250].

4. Divination. The Oedipus example is used against the Stoics: Apollo could not have foretold the parricide without prior natural causes. Stoics, who say all happens by fate, are therefore pressed harder than those who merely hold future truths to be eternally true [9c9b49da-3319-55f4-97d4-d3a99cd697c2].

Caveat: Carneades wrote nothing, and Amand conjectures that Antiochus of Ascalon is the immediate source [argument_carneades_autonomous_mental_causation_argument_4e7e9250].
