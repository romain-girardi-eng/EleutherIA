The KG supports a nuanced answer: continuity in argumentative structure, partial discontinuity in terminology and doctrinal setting.

Andresen (ZNW 44, 1952-53) is recorded as arguing continuity. He holds that Justin's antifatalism in 1 Apol. 43 structurally mirrors Alcinous, Didask. 26. If all things occur by heimarmene, to eph' hemin disappears, and so do praise and blame. The soul is free in its choices, while the consequences fall under fate [argument_justin_equals_alcinous_heimarmene; argument_justin_antifatalism; pub_andresen_1953_justin_platonismus]. Andresen places Justin in the "orthodox" Middle Platonism of Plutarch and Attikos [scholarly_argument_andresen_justin_s_philosophical_classif_0; scholarly_position_andresen_justin_middle_platonist]. Boys-Stones likewise counts Justin among witnesses to hypothetical fate [scholarly_argument_boys_stones_justin_martyr_as_middle_platon_7].

Karamanolis (ch. 4) is recorded as stressing distinctiveness. Early Christian thinkers reworked Greek notions autonomously rather than borrowing vocabulary [pub_karamanolis_2021_philosophy_early_christianity]. Scripture lacks the terminology, and to eph' hemin, prohairesis and autexousion are of Stoic provenance, taken up because Christians shared Stoic theological assumptions [scholarly_argument_karamanolis_free_will_and_divine_providenc_0]. The KG does not name Justin in that node, so applying it to him is my inference.

2 Apol. 5-7 is where Justin goes beyond the Platonist sources. He extends autexousion to angels, makes fate a demonic deception, and locates the origin of evil in free misuse [argument_justin_angel_fall]. Dial. 102.4 states that God made angels and humans autexousious for righteous action [passage 7b029b3b-d680-4362-a0ea-8ef4b2aef215]. Bobzien notes that autexousion is a term that later displaced eph' hemin, and that exousia does not occur in Justin's account of "what depends on us" [scholarly_argument_bobzien_autexousion_as_philosophical_t_6]. Frede traces the term to Stoic origin, popularized by Christians since Justin [argument_frede_2011_autexousion_stoic_origin_then_christian].
