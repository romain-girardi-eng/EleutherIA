Origen's argument (De Princ. III.1, the Peri autexousiou) runs in three moves. First, a classification of motions: inanimate things are moved only from outside, while living beings carry the cause of their motion in themselves [7e1c8576-c630-5099-8e98-082343bca3c6]. Second, a distinction between external events, which are "not in our power" (οὐκ ἔστιν ἐφ' ἡμῖν), and the judgement on how to use them, which belongs to "our self-determining judgment" (τῆς αὐτεξουσίου ἡμῶν κρίσεως) [3e767176-490c-58a3-a173-c7e10e5d85a9]. Third, an introspective reply to the determinist (III.1.4): nothing happens before the intellect assents and the will becomes complicit, as at a tribunal in the heart where reason gives the verdict [81e26db7-ddeb-5169-b72b-e64f7d1877f2]. Fürst reads this as aimed at three determinisms (astrological, Stoic-heimarmene, Gnostic) and says Origen accepts the causal framework while keeping genuine alternatives [argument_furst_2022_origen_against_three_determinisms].

Frede: the doctrine is "basically Stoic", explicated "along standard Stoic lines". Origen identifies freedom with there being things eph' hemin, and uses it not to correct the Stoics but against astral determinism and Gnosticism, within a Platonist outlook [scholarly_argument_frede_origen_s_doctrine_of_free_will_5]. Terminology and main theses are "through and through Stoic", mostly Epictetan. The departures (demons' freedom, fall and return, apokatastasis) come from Platonism, not Christianity [argument_frede_2011_origen_stoic_christianity_anti_gnostic].

Bobzien: the corpus holds no direct Bobzien text on Origen. She distinguishes the Stoic one-sided causative eph' hemin ("attributable to us") from the two-sided potestative sense, which itself entails neither determinism nor indeterminism [scholarly_argument_bobzien_eph_hemin_what_depends_on_us_3]. She denies that Epictetus introduces free will, resisting Frede's reading [argument_bobzien_2001_b1_epictetus_developmental_freedom]. This implies a more cautious view of continuity, but that is my inference, not attested.
