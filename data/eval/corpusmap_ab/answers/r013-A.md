Continuity is real on the core thesis, but Irenaeus adds a developmental frame Justin lacks. The KG node for AH Book 4 states that chapters 37-39 are the locus classicus of Irenaeus's αὐτεξούσιον and that he "builds directly on Justin Martyr's use of αὐτεξούσιον (1 Apol. 43-44; Dial. 141)" [work_irenaeus_adversus_haereses_book4]. (The node records no linked passages itself; the passage evidence comes from search.)

Shared points. (1) Justin: God willed angels and humans to be "ἐν ἐλευθέρᾳ προαιρέσει καὶ αὐτεξουσίους", rewarded or punished according to their choice [ad0049c4-8fa3-417c-9f02-42a98b0a862f]; also Dial. 141.1, God made them αὐτεξουσίους [9a8ffeae-8eab-445f-9475-5071fc3590f4]. Irenaeus likewise: God made man free from the beginning, with power of choice "quemadmodum et in angelis", and no compulsion from God [decb4efc-f086-5663-900a-635c9246b19e]; Greek fr.: "Βία Θεῷ οὐ πρόσεστιν" [f82703cb-03ab-594d-b492-60c15193a3d0]. (2) Praise and blame are just because all share one nature, able to keep or lose the good, not naturally good or bad [7c74409a-301c-5e6e-a0a8-ef71a7843cff; a2889e18-9207-5655-8dae-5deffb419d50]. Justin's argument that foreknowledge does not cause sin [9a8ffeae-...] is, in my reading, the same theodicy.

Differences. IV.37.4 reads Paul's "πάντα ἔξεστιν" as freedom without divine compulsion [4b73889d-a934-57c0-9af1-954c5ba76608]. The KG credits chapters 38-39 with the original νήπιος doctrine (Adam good but not yet perfect), which it presents as Irenaeus's sharpening of Justin [work_irenaeus_adversus_haereses_book4]. The retrieved Justin passages contain no such scheme. I did not retrieve IV.38-39 text itself, so that claim rests on the node description only. Verbal links such as Theophilus's ἐλεύθερον καὶ αὐτεξούσιον [b94d6360-df59-5628-ac3b-3c40f9eb0bbd] suggest a wider tradition, not a proven direct dependence.
