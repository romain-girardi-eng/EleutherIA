The KG records a patristic debate on freedom (autexousion) versus cosmic determinism between Christian theologians and Gnostic teachers [debate_christian_gnostic_freedom], linked to Origen and De Principiis [person_origen_alexandria_185_254ce_s9t0u1v2, work_de_principiis_origen_230s_v2w3x4y5], with opposed readings of Valentinian ethics: fixed natures excluding autexousia (Telfer) versus retained self-determination (Linjamaa) [position_telfer_valentinian_natural_determinism, position_linjamaa_valentinian_ethics_and_self_determination].

Second-century Apologists: Harl holds that from c. 150 they use autexousion in a quasi-technical sense for the Creator's "initial freedom" given to all creatures, framed against Gnostics who taught determined good or evil "natures" and left choice only to the "psychics" [scholarly_argument_harl_christian_choice; school_christian_apologists]. By contrast, Pouderon's summary has the Apologists fighting Gnostics over the Demiurge, Christ's humanity and the resurrection; it makes no claim about determinism or fate [scholarly_argument_pouderon_determinism_and_fate_vs_christ_1] (the node's label overstates this).

Origen: Sytsma presents his lifelong anti-determinism against Gnostic "natures", astral heimarmene and the Idle Argument, culminating in De Principiis III.1 [scholarly_argument_sytsma_anti_determinism_idle_argument_deprinc_iii]. De Princ. 3.1.1 grounds autexousion in the Church's preaching of just judgment [a343b50a-6de2-55be-b2d7-80e1c89133e5]. De Princ. 3.1.8 reports "heretics" who use Pharaoh's hardening to claim souls have natures that are saved or lost regardless [dc6ff8bc-31d6-52a9-a0c8-416df4587c99]. Contra Celsum II.20 answers the Idle Argument via Laius [8a7ecc77-2a73-5d39-8470-46b65ff495e0].

Caveat: Löhr treats Gnostic determinism as a heresiological cliché from Irenaeus through Clement to Origen, without judging its accuracy [scholarly_argument_l_hr_gnostic_determinism_as_heresio_0]. Direct Apologist passages were not retrieved.
