Freedom of the One. In Ennead VI.8 Plotinus asks whether autexousion and "what depends on us" can be said of the gods [2cef74de-5f4c-54e4-a117-9c91addb05fe]. He holds that self-mastery is not an accident of the First but is reached by removing the opposites that limit lower self-mastery [1019985d-051e-483c-9483-f9b60a748123]. Frede (2011, ch. 8) reads this as a four-level hierarchy of freedom: embodied human, soul, intellect, One. The One is the archetype, acting by an absolutely free and unconditioned will, while intellect is free because it cannot act otherwise [synthesis_frede2011_ch8_plotinus; argument_frede_2011_plotinus_hierarchized_freedom]. Leroux takes freedom to be univocal: each being's freedom to be what it is [scholarly_argument_leroux_freedom_univocal_necessity]. Narbonne argues the causa sui argument targets Gnostics. On his reading the One's "will" wills nothing and is self-determination rather than contingent choice [scholarly_argument_narbonne_god_as_causa_sui_and_self_dete_6]. Dihle holds that Plotinian will remains intellectualist: "will = activity of the nous", with no voluntary faculty distinct from intellect. On his view that step is Augustine's, so the rupture is Latin and Christian [argument_dihle_1982_plotinus_remains_intellectualist].

Patristic appropriation. Frede argues against Armstrong that divine free will is not specifically Judeo-Christian. At most he allows an Origen link through Ammonius Saccas and Porphyry [argument_frede_2011_plotinus_divine_will_not_judeo_christian].
