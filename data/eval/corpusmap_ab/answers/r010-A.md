The KG documents the Qumran side well but contains no attested link between 1QS and the Patristic predestination debate.

Qumran: the Treatise of the Two Spirits (1QS III.13-IV.26), embedded in the Community Rule [work_dss_community_rule_e7f8g9h0; concept_two_spirits_doctrine_s7t8u9v0], is described as the most sustained predestinarian text of Second Temple Judaism [argument_qumran_predestination_c3d4e5f6]. God fixed all design before creation (III.15), created the Spirits of Truth and Injustice and assigned each person to one by inheritance, not merit (III.18-19). Behind them stand the Prince of Light and the Angel of Darkness, who misleads even the sons of light (III.22-24); God will end injustice at the appointed end (IV.18-23). Scholars disagree: Lange reads strict double predestination, Frey a modified dualism leaving room for decision [argument_qumran_predestination_c3d4e5f6]. Brand sees a synthesis of internal and external sources of sin and of predestinarian and free-will views [scholarly_argument_brand_two_spirits_synthesizes_views], and ties free-will emphasis to genre [scholarly_argument_brand_genre_determines_free_will_view]. Klawans links Essene determinism (Josephus) to 1QS [scholarly_argument_klawans_2012_essene_determinism_matches_qumran; group_essenes_e9f0g1h2].

Patristic side: the KG models Augustine's predestination (election plus praeteritio of the reprobate) [concept_predestination_augustinian], with Ad Simplicianum as a source [work_ad_simplicianum] and the massa damnata [argument_augustine_massa_damnata].

Gap: the two-spirits node's neighbours include no Patristic node, the Qumran argument has no linked passages, and my passage search returned nothing relevant. Any claim of influence or direct comparison (for example Lange's "Calvinist" analogy aside) is therefore not attested in the corpus; at most a typological parallel can be drawn.
