Plotinus and Augustine both defend a real freedom of the soul against determinism, but they ground it differently. The evidence below comes from KG node descriptions only. The corpus returned no ancient passages for these works, so I cannot quote the primary texts.

Plotinus. In Ennead III.1 he rejects Epicurean atomism, astral determinism and Stoic total causal interconnection, because they dissolve agency [work_plotinus_enn_iii_1]. Freedom is the soul's self-mastery. Disembodied, the soul is fully self-determining; embodied, it is exposed to circumstance but can resist it. Genuinely voluntary action (hekousion) occurs only when the soul acts from purified reason, free of ignorance and appetite [work_plotinus_enn_iii_1]. Graeser reads this as freedom on "two levels". The empirical self is determined in a Stoic manner, and the non-empirical, metaphysical self is the independent principle [scholarly_argument_graeser_plotinus_critique_of_stoic_com_1]. VI.8 transposes autexousion to the One, as absolute self-necessity [work_plotinus_ennead_vi_8_d8b9c5a4].

Augustine. In De libero arbitrio the will is the proximate cause of moral evil, through aversion from eternal to temporal goods; evil is a privation [work_de_libero_arbitrio]. Nothing is so much in our power as the will itself (III.3.7) [work_de_libero_arbitrio]. Book III defends the compatibility of divine foreknowledge with agency, and inherited weakness is traced to Adam [work_de_libero_arbitrio]. In Confessions VIII the will is divided against itself: the two wills are a self-divided will, not reason against passion [work_augustine_confessiones_viii]. In the mature anti-Pelagian De gratia et libero arbitrio, free will is real but needs grace to choose the good, and even the good will is a gift [work_augustine_de_gratia_la].

Contrast. Plotinus locates freedom in the soul's return to reason and in the One's self-necessity. Augustine locates it in a wounded will that depends on grace. The corpus does not attest a direct Plotinian influence on Augustine's doctrine of the will.
