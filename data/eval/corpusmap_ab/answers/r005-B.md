The corpus does not explicitly pose or settle the "genuine controversy vs. rhetorical exercise" dichotomy, and no linked passages for the work were returned [work_methodius_de_libero_arbitrio]. The evidence available leans toward genuine controversy, with a qualification.
For genuine: Amand 1945 presents Methodius as Origen's principal intellectual adversary and organizer of the conservative reaction, opposing creation ab aeterno, pre-existence and fall of souls, and an over-spiritualized resurrection [synthesis_amand1945_methodius_anti_origenist_reaction]. The treatise is described as the earliest Christian work devoted to defending free will (autexousion), with an anti-Gnostic theodicy: evil comes neither from matter nor from God but from misuse of human choice [argument_methodius_theodicy_autexousion]. Its stress on freedom as the gift of uncoerced obedience, against instrumentalism, follows Irenaeus [argument_methodius_libre_arbitre_obeissance_irenean].
For a scholastic/rhetorical dimension: Amand holds that Methodius took three Neo-Academic syllogisms (Symposium VIII.16.227-229) probably from a school manual (hypomnema), transcribing them almost literally without Christian transposition [synthesis_amand1945_methodius_indirect_via_hypomnema; argument_methodius_symposium_three_carneadean_syllogisms]. That concerns borrowed argumentative material, not insincerity.
Caveat: the targets in the cited material are Gnostic/dualist (matter as origin of evil) and Origenist theses in general; the corpus does not show that the De Autexusio's free-will argument itself is aimed specifically at Origen. Beyond this, unknown.
