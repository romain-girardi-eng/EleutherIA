# Arbitration — ἦν ποτε ὅτε οὐκ ἦν: from Peripatetic commonplace to Arian slogan

**Method:** whole-corpus attestation scan of the TLG-E (`scripts/tlg_search.py`, accent-insensitive), no
interpretive leap — pure philology, in the Amand-Carneades precedent. **Date:** 2026-06-15.

**Question (from the Alexander debate, G5):** Ramelli (2014) holds that the formula ἦν ποτε ὅτε οὐκ ἦν ("there was a
time when it was not") "was used for the first time exactly by Alexander and Origen" — evidence for *Alexander as a
source feeding Christian thought*, against Bobzien's reading of Alexander as a "dead-end."

## What the corpus shows

| Author (TLG) | Hits | Register | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Alexander of Aphrodisias** (TLG0732, 2nd–3rd c. CE) | **3** | **philosophical** | The formula expresses temporal non-eternity / coming-to-be: *"οὐ γὰρ ἀεὶ ἦν ὁ γραμματικὸς μουσικός, ἀλλ' ἦν ποτε ὅτε οὐκ ἦν"* ("the grammarian was not always musical, but there was a time when he was not"); and defining the eternal (ἀίδιον) as that of which one cannot say ἦν ποτε ὅτε οὐκ ἦν. |
| **Origen** (TLG2042, 3rd c.) | **1** | **theological (contra)** | Already engages it about the Son — arguing *against*: *"κατανοείτω… ὁ τολμῶν καὶ λέγων· ἦν ποτε ὅτε οὐκ ἦν ὁ υἱός, ὅτι ἐρεῖ καὶ τό· σοφία ποτὲ οὐκ ἦν…"* (a pre-Arian theological deployment). |
| **Athanasius** (TLG2035, 4th c.) | ~20+ | theological (anti-Arian) | The formula as the central Arian slogan being refuted. |
| Eusebius, Epiphanius, Gregory of Nyssa (4th c.) | several each | theological | Creedal anathemas ("τοὺς δὲ λέγοντας ἦν ποτε ὅτε οὐκ ἦν… ἀναθεματίζει") — the Nicene condemnation. |

## Verdict

**Arbitrable and largely CONFIRMED — with a precise scope limit.**

1. **CONFIRMED:** Alexander of Aphrodisias demonstrably uses ἦν ποτε ὅτε οὐκ ἦν as a *philosophical* commonplace
   (non-eternity / generation), the earliest attestations in the formula's documented trajectory. Ramelli's
   "Alexander used it" is true at the level of attestation.
2. **CONFIRMED:** Origen already deploys it *theologically* (about the Son, contra) in the 3rd c., i.e. **before** the
   Arian controversy — establishing a pre-Arian theological history, not a 4th-c. coinage *ex nihilo*.
3. **TRAJECTORY ESTABLISHED:** Peripatetic philosophical formula (Alexander) → early Christological engagement
   (Origen) → Arian slogan condemned at Nicaea (Athanasius et al.). This **materially supports** "Alexander's
   vocabulary reaches the Christian debate" and **complicates Bobzien's "dead-end"** — for *this* lexeme, Alexander
   demonstrably feeds the Christian theological lexicon.

**Honest limit (what the corpus does NOT settle):** the scan proves *lexical* transmission of one formula about
*eternity/generation*, not *conceptual* influence on free-will doctrine (αὐτεξούσιον) specifically, and not a direct
Alexander→Origen citation (shared vocabulary ≠ proven dependence). It is one corroborating thread in the larger
Alexander-influence case, not a proof of the free-will thesis. The verdict is a *philological* arbitration; the
*conceptual* disputes (is Alexander a libertarian? did he influence Origen's doctrine of freedom?) remain
underdetermined and rest on each scholar's criteria, which no text referees.

**Reproducibility:** `python3 scripts/tlg_search.py search "ἦν ποτε ὅτε οὐκ ἦν"` (+ `--authors 2042` for Origen).
All positions kept attributed; no ancient text fabricated.
