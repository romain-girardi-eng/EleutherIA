# G5 — Consensus / Contested Matrix: αὐτεξούσιον and τὸ ἐφ' ἡμῖν

Pairwise scholar-stance matrix over the two key Greek terms and the shared ancient loci. Cells use three relations:
- **AGREE** — same reading/claim;
- **CONTEST** — explicit or substantive disagreement;
- **EXTEND** — accepts the other's core point but pushes further / relocates / refines.

All modern labels are attributed, not asserted. Sourced from `positions_ephhemin_autexousion.jsonl` (primary), with cross-references to `positions_stoic_freewill.jsonl`, `positions_origins_will.jsonl`, `positions_alexander.jsonl`.

Scholars in scope: **Bobzien** (1998/2001), **Frede** (M., 2011), **Eliasson** (2008), **Labarrière** (2009), **Long & Sedley** (1987). Sorabji, Inwood, Salles, Brennan, Fürst included as cross-debate anchors where they touch the same loci.

## 1. τὸ ἐφ' ἡμῖν — "is it free will?"

Shared loci: Aristotle *NE* III.5 (1113b5–8); Chrysippus via Cic. *De fato* 40–43 + Gellius *NA* 7.2; Epictetus *Diss.*; Alexander *De fato* 192.22ff.

|  | Bobzien | Frede (M.) | Eliasson | Labarrière |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bobzien** | — one-sided causative (Chrysippus) vs two-sided potestative; "free will" begs the question; indeterminist reading only in Alexander | **CONTEST** on *when* free will first appears (B: Alexander; F: Epictetus). **AGREE** *eph' hēmin* ≠ free will | **AGREE** — Eliasson adopts Bobzien's one-/two-sided framework explicitly | **AGREE** — both reject the anachronistic "free will" rendering |
| **Frede (M.)** | **CONTEST** (Alexander vs Epictetus) / **AGREE** *eph' hēmin* ≠ free will | — *eph' hēmin* is a *precondition*; free will needs will + *eleutheria*; first in Epictetus, not Alexander | **AGREE** that the terms were distinct issues; **EXTEND**: Frede locates a positive emergence (Epictetus) where Eliasson stays descriptive | **AGREE** on non-anachronistic reading |
| **Eliasson** | **AGREE / EXTEND** — applies Bobzien to Plotinus; stresses 4 distinct notions | **AGREE** they were separate notions; neutral on Frede's positive Epictetus claim | — translating any as "free will" collapses real distinctions; criticizes even Sharples' "libertarian/soft-determinist" gloss as too narrow | **AGREE** — shared anti-anachronism |
| **Labarrière** | **AGREE** | **AGREE** | **AGREE** | — resists modern "subjectivity/liberty"; flags Gauthier's *autexousion* dating as Christianizing |

**Cross-debate anchors on this term:**
- **Inwood 1985** (Debate A): AGREE with Bobzien — Stoic *eph' hēmin* ≠ Alexander's "either of two opposites"; rejects "free will" reading of Cleanthes' *Hymn*.
- **Brennan / Salles** (Debate A): AGREE — "up to us" = originating from one's own character (compatibilist), no libertarian power to do otherwise.
- **Bobzien on Aristotle** (D, p.142–143): Aristotle's *eph' hēmin* is two-sided but **neutral** toward indeterminism — an internal refinement bearing on Irwin's "Aristotle already has the will" (Debate B): they CONTEST the upshot (Irwin: rational-desire will already present; Bobzien: two-sided ≠ will/indeterminism).

**Net on τὸ ἐφ' ἡμῖν:** four-way **consensus** that it is not "free will" and that the translation begs the question. The **single contested axis** is *when* a genuine free-will notion first appears — Bobzien (Alexander, and only "seemingly"/inadvertently) vs Frede (Epictetus, genuinely).

## 2. τὸ αὐτεξούσιον — origin, equation with *eph' hēmin*, Christianization

Shared loci: Musonius fr. 12/16; Epictetus *Diss.* II.2.3, IV.1.56–100; Alexander *De fato* XIV; Nemesius *De nat. hom.* 39 (311.7 Morani); Justin *1 Apol.* 43–44; Origen *De princ.* III.1.

### 2a. Origin of the term

|  | Bobzien | Frede (M.) | Eliasson | Labarrière |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bobzien** | — legal/political → philosophical (Musonius/Epictetus), 1st c. | **AGREE / EXTEND** (Frede stresses Stoic channel specifically) | **AGREE** | **AGREE** (both note pre-technical 1st-c. BCE attestations) |
| **Frede (M.)** | **AGREE** | — Stoic-derived; attested in Musonius, frequent in Epictetus; "ability to act of one's own initiative", not "having a free will" | **AGREE** | **AGREE** |
| **Eliasson** | **AGREE** | **AGREE** | — legal/political (released POWs; God's power) → philosophical jargon (Musonius/Epictetus); flags the SVF II.975 Zeno/Chrysippus attribution as **disputed** (with Bobzien) | **AGREE / CONTEST** on the precise first-technical dating (Gauthier: Epictetus; others: Musonius/Chrysippus) |
| **Labarrière** | **AGREE** | **AGREE** | **CONTEST (minor)** on dating — cites Gauthier (Epictetus) but flags Gauthier's Christianizing lens | — earliest non-technical example Diodorus Siculus 19.105.4; technical use fixed by Epictetus; *liberum arbitrium* only later (Jerome) |

### 2b. Equation *autexousion* = *eph' hēmin*

|  | Bobzien | Frede (M.) | Eliasson |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bobzien** | — *autexousion* supersedes *eph' hēmin* from late 2nd c., synonymous by Nemesius | **AGREE / EXTEND** | **AGREE** — same endpoint (Nemesius) |
| **Frede (M.)** | **AGREE** | — Clement still uses *eph' hēmin*; Origen titles treatise *Peri autexousiou* but defines via *eph' hēmin* (the transitional moment) | **AGREE** — compatible with Eliasson's 3-stage scheme |
| **Eliasson** | **AGREE** | **AGREE / EXTEND** (Eliasson adds the explicit 3-stage: Epictetus distinct → Alexander equates → Nemesius synonymous) | — Alexander is "the first to clearly state that αὐτεξούσιον is what ἐφ' ἡμῖν refers to" |

### 2c. Christianization (who first, continuous or discontinuous?)

|  | Bobzien | Frede (M.) | Eliasson |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bobzien** | — Christian adoption continuous with Hellenistic-Imperial development; Christian problem is *theological* (providence/evil), distinct from Alexander's physical-causal one | **AGREE** continuity; **EXTEND**: Frede pins first Christian user = Justin Martyr; Stoic-via-Platonist channel | **AGREE** — no discontinuous Christian invention |
| **Frede (M.)** | **AGREE** | — Justin first; Tatian coins *eleutheria tēs prohaireseōs*; Origen first systematic, "along standard Stoic lines"; even Methodius found no fault | **AGREE** — same trajectory and figures |
| **Eliasson** | **AGREE** | **AGREE** | — *autexousion* "becomes adopted by Christians" (Methodius, Origen, Hippolytus); same pagan trajectory, no rupture |

**Cross-debate anchors on this term:**
- **Frede** (Debate B, p.121): Origen's *deviations* from Chrysippus/Epictetus come from **Platonism**, not Christianity → Origen a transmitter (CONTEST with Fürst).
- **Fürst 2022** (Debate B, p.178): **EXTEND/CONTEST** — accepts the Stoic-terminology reception but relocates the decisive step: Origen "first systematically conceived" libertarianism (*kompatibilistischer Libertarismus*), a substantive Christian advance, not mere transmission.
- **Ramelli 2014** (Debate C): **EXTEND** — Alexander a positive *source* of Origen's *autexousion* (read in Plotinus' school), against Bobzien's "stops short" and M. Frede's "incoherent ancestor".
- **Long & Sedley 1987** (vol. II, 310): supplies the *apo* vs *di* agency-marker distinction Labarrière uses to read Alexander's appropriation of Stoic terms — a philological sub-claim, not contested by the others.

**Net on τὸ αὐτεξούσιον:** near-total **consensus** on origin (legal/political → Stoic philosophical), gradual equation with *eph' hēmin* (complete in Nemesius), and continuous (non-invented) Christian adoption along the Justin → Origen → Methodius → Nemesius line. The **two live contests** sit at the edges: (a) precise first-technical dating of the term (minor: Epictetus vs Musonius/Chrysippus, SVF II.975 disputed); (b) whether Origen merely *transmits* a Stoic notion (Frede) or *systematically invents* libertarian self-determination (Fürst) — and, behind it, whether Alexander is a dead-end (Bobzien/M. Frede) or a live source (Ramelli).
