## Bibliographic / authorship note (integrity)
- The book is signed **"DAVID AMAND, Maredsous, le 6 décembre 1944"** (avant-propos, p. XIII; OCR line 372–373). "David" is the author's monastic name; he later wrote as **Emmanuel Amand de Mendieta**. The Hakkert 1973 catalogue entry uses "Amand de Mendieta, Emmanuel" — same person. The substance is a 1945 Louvain doctoral thesis; the WWII context is explicit (avant-propos closes by apologising for not following Anglo-Saxon scholarship "que la guerre mondiale m'a empêché de me tenir au courant", p. XIII; the printer's troubles "dès la fin de 1943", p. XII).
- **Method is self-described as "strictement philologique et historique", "principalement documentaire"** (avant-propos, p. X, OCR 250–252), NOT a synthetic philosophy of free will. He explicitly warns the title could mislead anyone seeking "un exposé synthétique, conçu d'un point de vue philosophique" (p. X).

## HEADLINE THESIS
A single, identifiable polemical schema — **the *moral* anti-fatalist argument that Carneades sharpened and popularised against Chrysippean/Stoic εἱμαρμένη** — survived, was reused, summarised, transposed and sometimes reproduced almost verbatim, by Greek philosophers AND by the Greek Christian theologians of the first four centuries (Philo, Alexander of Aphrodisias, Eusebius, Origen, Gregory of Nyssa, Chrysostom, Nemesius, etc.). The book traces this *Nachleben*, isolates six developed "textes témoins," and from their convergence offers a **conjectural, fragmentary reconstruction** of the lost Carneadean moral argument (theme + 5/6 reconstituted arguments).

> "il nous fut aisé de l'identifier : c'était celle que Carnéade avait sinon inventée, du moins aiguisée et popularisée dans sa lutte contre le dogmatisme stoïcien." (avant-propos, p. IX, OCR 215–217)
> EN: "it was easy for us to identify it: it was the [argument] that Carneades had, if not invented, at least sharpened and popularised in his struggle against Stoic dogmatism."

Bears directly on **Debate E (Carneadean transmission to the Christian free-will tradition)** and **Debate D (the *Wortgeschichte* / attestation question)**. The quick-pass map already invokes this book as the "**Amand–Carneades precedent**" — the model of a contested attribution settled by checking what the ancient texts actually say. That methodological framing is faithful (see §"Quick-pass check" below).

## CLAIM-BY-CLAIM DOSSIER

### Claim 1 — Carneades is the *fons et origo* of the surviving anti-astrological/anti-fatalist polemic; the pre-Carneadean polemic (Aristotle, Epicurus, Cynics) left no real trace.
- **Page:** 41 (OCR 3300–3319).
- **Verbatim anchor (FR):** "La polémique qu'ont menée Aristote, Épicure, Antisthène et Diogène en faveur de la liberté humaine et contre le fatalisme stoïcien, n'eut guère de répercussion sur les philosophes postérieurs… Il en va autrement des âpres attaques… que Carnéade, le fondateur de la Nouvelle Académie (214/213–129/128 avant J.-C.), lança contre le fatalisme absolu de Chrysippe." (p. 41)
  - EN: "The polemic conducted by Aristotle, Epicurus, Antisthenes and Diogenes in favour of human freedom and against Stoic fatalism had scarcely any repercussion on later philosophers… It is otherwise with the bitter attacks… that Carneades, the founder of the New Academy (214/213–129/128 BC), launched against the absolute fatalism of Chrysippus."
- **Carneades wrote nothing (transmission is *oral* → Clitomachus → lost):** "Carnéade n'a rien écrit. Son enseignement fut exclusivement oral, comme celui de Socrate. Il créa une série de τόποι ou d'arguments qu'il inventa ou auxquels il se borna à conférer une forme dialectique incisive." (p. 41, OCR 3309–3312). **"Carnéade fixa donc les traits essentiels de la polémique antifataliste chez les Grecs et les Latins."** (p. 41).
- **Hedged on invention:** Amand consistently writes "inventa **ou** … se borna à conférer une forme dialectique incisive" / "sinon inventée, du moins aiguisée et popularisée." He does **not** assert Carneades invented the topoi outright — only that he gave them their classic incisive form and disseminated them.
- **Ancient locus:** Cicero, *De fato*, the Aulus Gellius testimony (XIV.1.3-4, ed. Hosius II, p. 103), *Mathesis* of Firmicus Maternus.
- **Agrees/contests:** sits within the source-critical tradition (cites Schmekel, Lörcher — "très solide et très méthodique" — Pohlenz, Yon, Gercke) that Cicero's *De fato* depends, probably directly, on **Antiochus of Ascalon** (p. 66 n.2, OCR 4483–4500). Frames Carneades→Augustine as the historical arc: the polemic "inaugurée par Carnéade et qu'Augustin devait clore en la résumant" (p. 42, OCR 3346–3347).

### Claim 2 — Carneades' conception of liberty is *pragmatist/probabilist*, NOT a dogmatic libertarianism; this is what distinguishes him from the later Christian/Neoplatonist champions of free will.
- **Page:** 65–66 (OCR 4420–4457).
- **Verbatim anchor (FR):** "fidèle à son probabilisme, Carnéade n'érige pas la liberté humaine en dogme, ni ne la présente comme une indubitable certitude. Il soutient seulement … contre Chrysippe, Antipatros et toute l'école stoïcienne, la haute probabilité, la vraisemblance d'une proposition… on doit dire que c'est nous-mêmes qui sommes la cause de ces volitions, et qu'elles ne relèvent pas de causes extérieures antécédentes." (p. 65)
  - EN: "faithful to his probabilism, Carneades does not erect human liberty into a dogma, nor present it as an indubitable certainty. He maintains only… against Chrysippus, Antipater and the whole Stoic school, the high probability, the likelihood of a proposition… one must say that it is we ourselves who are the cause of these volitions, and that they do not derive from antecedent external causes."
- **Sharp contrast asserted by Amand:** "Ce subtil dialecticien, ce demi-sceptique n'est point un moraliste… ni un champion acharné du libre arbitre. C'est avant tout un critique, et c'est ce qui le distingue profondément des théologiens néo-platoniciens et chrétiens, essentiellement dogmatistes, ardents défenseurs de la liberté humaine… **Origène sera le champion du libre arbitre**… avec une chaleur, une insistance, une conviction totale et passionnée, que l'on chercherait en vain dans les disputes publiques de l'adversaire de Chrysippe." (p. 65–66, OCR 4432–4443).
- **Ancient locus:** Cicero *De fato* 6,11 (ed. Yon p.6); *De fato* 10,20–12,28, esp. §23; cf. 9,19–20; 17,39 / 18,41 / 19,43 (ed. Yon). Carneades, like Epicurus, admits "un mouvement pleinement volontaire et la liberté du vouloir," but **against** Epicurus keeps "rien sans cause" and the law of contradictories/excluded middle: voluntary motion ≠ uncaused motion; its principle is our will (p. 67, OCR 4504–4525).
- **Agrees/contests:** explicitly endorses **L. Robin** (*La morale antique* pp. 165–167) and **J. Croissant** on Carneades' "pragmatist" original position "à la fois contre l'indéterminisme total… d'Épicure et… contre le déterminisme fataliste des Stoïciens" (p. 67 n.2, OCR 4534–4539); refers to **V. Brochard**, *Les sceptiques grecs* pp. 148–153, as the fine analysis of "l'admirable discussion de Carnéade sur le libre arbitre" (p. 66 n.2, OCR 4493–4497).
- **NOTE (integrity / attribution):** the modern-flavoured labels here ("probabilisme," "pragmatiste," "demi-sceptique," "libre arbitre," "indéterminisme") are **Amand's own characterisation** (1945), themselves leaning on Robin/Brochard. He does NOT use "libertarian" or "compatibilism."

### Claim 3 — Methodological "règle de fer": an argument counts as Carneadean iff attested by ≥3 of the 6 *textes témoins*.
- **Page:** 572–573 (OCR 29398–29425).
- **The six *textes témoins*** (Conclusion, OCR 29340–29371, with their internal page-refs to the book): (1) **Philon**, *Περὶ προνοίας* I,77–88; (2) **Alexandre d'Aphrodise**, *Περὶ εἱμαρμένης* (*De fato*) 16–20; (3) **Firmicus Maternus**, *Mathesis* I,2; (4) **Eusèbe de Césarée**, *Préparation évangélique* VI,6; (5) **Jean Chrysostome**, *Hom. après le prêtre Goth* 6 (PG 63,500–510); (6) **ps.-Chrysostome**, *Discours sur le Destin et la Providence* V (PG 50,765–768). Confirmatory texts: Bardesane (*Livre des lois des pays*), Basile (*Hex.* VI,7), the Arian Job-commentator, Nemesius (*De nat. hom.* 35).
- **Verbatim anchor (FR):** "**Est censé carnéadien tout argument attesté par 3 témoins au moins sur 6.** Quant aux autres arguments conservés dans un nombre inférieur de témoins, chaque cas devra être examiné séparément." (p. 573, OCR 29414–29417).
  - EN: "Any argument attested by at least 3 of the 6 witnesses is deemed Carneadean. As for the other arguments preserved in a smaller number of witnesses, each case must be examined separately."
- **Self-qualification (repeated, emphatic):** the whole reconstruction is "toute conjecturale… ne pourra guère revendiquer qu'une haute probabilité" (p. 572, OCR 29381–29384). This honesty IS the "Amand–Carneades precedent" the quick-pass map relies on.

### Claim 4 — The reconstructed Carneadean moral argument = a *theme* + five/six reductio-ad-absurdum arguments.
- **Page:** 581–584 (OCR 29739–29837).
- **Theme (p. 582):** denying human freedom and accepting Chrysippean astral fatalism "c'est d'introduire la confusion et le bouleversement dans la vie morale" — no virtue/vice, no praise/blame, laws & penal repression useless, ruin of piety/religion, all incentives to action void, end-point = total inaction. (OCR 29756–29768).
- The five reconstructed arguments (p. 582–584): (1) **laws, courts, penalties must be abolished**, the criminal is an irresponsible "passif instrument" of the planets; (2) **virtue/vice distinction collapses** (no sense, no merit); (3) **praise, exhortation, reward / blame, reproach, punishment all pointless** — yet "s'expliquent naturellement dans le cas du libre arbitre" (OCR 29803–29804); (4) **belief in εἱμαρμένη → relaxation of effort, sloth**, neglect of virtue, indifference to children's education and slaves' conduct (these three sub-cases flagged "faiblement attestés," n.1 p. 584, OCR 29856–29861); (5) **fatalism ruins piety / prayer / cult** (the planets/gods cannot help, prayers cannot bend εἱμαρμένη). (Numbering: he lists theme + 5 numbered points, the last under heading "6)" — i.e. theme=1, then 2–6.)
- **Ancient loci (each point cross-keyed):** e.g. theme ← Alexander *De fato* 16 & 20, Firmicus *Math.* I,2,5, Eusebius *PE* VI,6,5, Chrysostom Goth-hom. 6, ps.-Chrys. *De fato* V; "laws/courts" ← Philo *De prov.* I,79–82, Firmicus *Math.* I,2,10-11, Eusebius *PE* VI,6,18, Basile *Hex.* VI,7, Nemesius *De nat. hom.* 35; "piety/prayer" ← Alexander *De fato* 17, Firmicus *Math.* I,2,8-9, Eusebius *PE* VI,6,19, Nemesius *De nat. hom.* 85 (OCR 29429–29737).

### Claim 5 — Three further arguments are attested *only / chiefly by Alexander of Aphrodisias* (De fato 17–19) and are *probably* but not certainly Carneadean.
- **Page:** 584–586 (OCR 29838–29913).
- Content: (a) the fatalist must deny divine Providence and abolish μαντική (De fato 17, complement to argument 6); (b) the Stoics' *practical conduct* (teaching, reproaching, exhorting, writing for pupils) contradicts their fatalist theory — moral action itself reveals "le fait de notre autonomie interne" (De fato 18; paralleled by Eusebius *PE* VI,6,16-17, who "ne nomme pas les Stoïciens"); (c) the Stoics in practice judge criminals worthy of punishment instead of pitying them as fated (De fato 19).
- **Verbatim anchor (FR):** "Il est impossible de décider si le long raisonnement développé par Alexandre d'Aphrodise reproduit l'argument de Carnéade tel quel, ou bien s'il constitue une expansion, une amplification scolaire de la preuve originelle. En tout cas, la provenance carnéadienne de l'essentiel de l'argument paraît très probable." (p. 586, OCR 29909–29913).
  - EN: "It is impossible to decide whether the long reasoning developed by Alexander of Aphrodisias reproduces Carneades' argument as such, or whether it constitutes a scholastic expansion/amplification of the original proof. In any case, the Carneadean provenance of the essential of the argument seems very probable."
- **Bears on Debate D/E:** Amand REPORTS Alexander as the richest single witness but does **not** claim Alexander as *the* source of the Christians; the common ancestor is the (lost) Carneadean/Clitomachean stock, transmitted partly via Antiochus/Cicero and the Jewish apologetic (Philo). He treats Alexander as a *parallel witness*, not an intermediary link — relevant if the corpus debate (Ramelli vs Bobzien) tries to make Alexander the conduit to Origen.

### Claim 6 (Debate E core) — Origen receives and *Christianises* the Carneadean moral argument, transposing the topos into the eschatological/theodicy register.
- **Page:** 321–333 (esp. 322, 332–333) (OCR 16898–16984).
- **Verbatim anchor (FR):** "**Dans l'argument suivant, Origène se rapproche de Carnéade.** Celui-ci avait montré que, si l'on tient pour vrai le fatalisme astrologique, il faut abroger comme inutiles et injustes lois, tribunaux et sanctions judiciaires, qu'il faut déclarer irresponsables les pires criminels… Fidèle à la tradition héritée de l'apologétique juive, le théologien chrétien complète la preuve par une considération relevant de la théodicée. En faisant retomber la responsabilité des crimes sur le Destin, c'est en définitive Dieu lui-même que l'on accuse d'être l'auteur du mal." (p. 322, OCR 16908–16920).
  - EN: "In the following argument, Origen draws close to Carneades. The latter had shown that, if astrological fatalism is held true, laws, courts and judicial sanctions must be repealed as useless and unjust, and the worst criminals declared irresponsible… Faithful to the tradition inherited from Jewish apologetic, the Christian theologian completes the proof with a consideration of theodicy. By throwing the responsibility for crimes onto Fate, it is ultimately God himself whom one accuses of being the author of evil."
- **Transposition explicitly flagged:** "Origène transpose le τόπος carnéadien, en le plaçant dans la perspective chrétienne de l'au-delà… : les punitions des pécheurs, les récompenses… réservées à l'autre vie." (p. 321 n.1, OCR 16898–16901).
- **Ancient locus + verbatim Greek (present in file, from Robinson's edition):** Origen, **Philocalie 23,1**, ed. J. A. Robinson p. 188, ll. 16–29:
  > "Πυθοίμεθα δ᾽ ἂν αὐτῶν, τί ὁ θεὸς βουλόμενος τοιοῦτον ἐποίει κόσμον, ἵν᾽ οἱ μὲν ἐν αὐτῷ ἄνδρες ὄντες τὰ γυναικῶν πάσχωσιν, οὐδαμῶς ἑαυτοῖς αἴτιοι τῆς ἀσελγείας γεγενημένοι…" (OCR 16945–16956)
  - Origen's broader corpus on prescience vs free will: **Philocalie 23** (= *Contra Celsum* / *Comm. in Gen.*) and the prescience discussion (sections 3–5, four problems on divine πρόγνωσις and genethlialogy, OCR 16296–16392), where Amand argues Origen holds that **divine foreknowledge leaves τὸ ἐφ᾽ ἡμῖν wholly intact** ("la prescience de Dieu laisse absolument intacte" human freedom, OCR 16341).
- **"libre arbitre, dogme chrétien":** Amand frames Origen's free will as raised to the level of a Christian dogma and "pièce maîtresse de sa philosophie religieuse" (table p. 297-298, OCR 955; p. 66 OCR 4439–4441).
- **Agrees/contests:** within the chapter Amand engages de Faye, H. Koch, E. Schwartz on Origen's churchly character; on the antifatalist argument he claims to be the **first** to give "une analyse assez poussée de la copieuse dissertation antiastrologique d'Origène" (Épilogue p. 589, OCR 30008–30009).

### Claim 7 — Gregory of Nyssa's *Contra Fatum*: Amand offers a *working hypothesis* (not a proof) that its reductio arguments derive, directly or not, from the Carneadean stock.
- **Page:** 589–590 (Épilogue) (OCR 30038–30050).
- **Verbatim anchor (FR):** "Nous avons émis une hypothèse de travail : c'est que la plupart de ces arguments ad absurdum ont été empruntés par Grégoire à une source littéraire représentant directement ou non la polémique antifataliste de Carnéade." (p. 590, OCR 30047–30050).
  - EN: "We have advanced a working hypothesis: that most of these *ad absurdum* arguments were borrowed by Gregory from a literary source representing, directly or not, the anti-fatalist polemic of Carneades."
- Marks the careful asserts/reports boundary: Gregory's Carneadean derivation is offered as *hypothesis*, Eusebius'/Chrysostom's near-verbatim reproduction as near-*fact* ("reproduite telle quelle, du moins dans ses raisonnements essentiels, par un Eusèbe de Césarée et par un Jean Chrysostome," p. 590, OCR 30060–30063).

### Claim 8 (cultural thesis) — The reuse of a pagan Academic argument by Christian theologians evidences the *homogeneity* of Greco-Roman intellectual culture; "profane" and Christian literature should not be studied in sealed compartments.
- **Page:** 587–591 (Épilogue).
- **Verbatim anchor (FR):** "à savoir l'homogénéité de la civilisation intellectuelle gréco-romaine, homogénéité maintenue même après la diffusion du christianisme… Il se persuadera que le temps est désormais passé où l'on étudiait à part et comme en cloisons étanches littérature « profane » et littérature chrétienne." (p. 591, OCR 30087–30092).
- Subsidiary historical claim: all ancient philosophical schools **except the Stoics, the intermediate/Middle Platonists, and the Plotinian Neoplatonists** united against εἱμαρμένη (Épilogue p. 587, 590, OCR 29937–29940 & 30035–30036).

## ASSERT vs REPORT — boundary summary
- **Amand ASSERTS:** Carneades as origin/popularizer of the surviving moral antifatalist topoi; Carneades' liberty = probabilist/pragmatist not dogmatic; the ≥3/6 reconstruction rule; the theme + 5/6 reconstructed arguments; the homogeneity-of-culture thesis; Origen/Eusebius/Chrysostom as genuine bearers of the topos.
- **Amand explicitly REPORTS / HEDGES:** whether Carneades literally *invented* (vs sharpened) the topoi; whether Alexander *De fato* 17–19 reproduces Carneades verbatim or amplifies him ("impossible de décider"); the Gregory-of-Nyssa derivation (explicit "hypothèse de travail"); the whole reconstruction as "conjecturale … haute probabilité."
- **Amand REPORTS others' positions:** Robin & Croissant & Brochard on Carneades' pragmatism (endorsed); Lörcher/Schmekel/Yon/Gercke on the De fato → Antiochus dependence (leans in favour); de Faye / H. Koch / Schwartz on Origen's churchly identity.

## Quick-pass map check (`data/goals/g5/`)
- The quick-pass map does **not** profile Amand's substantive positions; it cites him only as the methodological **"Amand–Carneades precedent"** in `arbitrable_debates.md` (l. 3, 61) and `arbitration_hen_pote_ote_ouk_en.md` (l. 4): *"a contested attribution decided by checking what the ancient text actually says against the corpus."*
- **Verdict: NOT a misrepresentation.** This is a fair, even apt, distillation of Amand's actual method (his self-described "méthode strictement philologique et historique," p. X, and the ≥3/6 *règle de fer*, p. 573). The reconstruction proceeds exactly by comparing what the *textes témoins* literally attest.
- **One nuance the map should carry if it leans on Amand for Debate C/D:** Amand treats **Alexander of Aphrodisias as a *parallel witness* to the lost Carneadean stock, NOT as the proximate *source* of Origen.** He never asserts an Alexander→Origen filiation; he is explicit (p. 586) that he cannot decide whether Alexander even reproduces Carneades verbatim. So Amand is **neutral-to-unsupportive** of any "Alexander is the source of Origen" influence claim (the Ramelli-type thesis the map flags for arbitration). His framework would route both Alexander and Origen back to Carneades/Clitomachus (with Cicero/Antiochus and Philo's Jewish apologetic as channels), not Alexander→Origen. The map currently does not over-claim here, but if Amand is ever cited *for* Ramelli's priority thesis that would be a misuse.
