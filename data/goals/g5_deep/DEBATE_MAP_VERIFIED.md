## DEBATE A — Did the early Stoa have a notion of *free will*? What is *to eph' hēmin*?

The question is asked three ways; the answers split differently on each:
1. **Libertarian/indeterminist free will in the early Stoa?** — near-unanimous **NO** (Bobzien, Inwood, Brennan, Salles, Frede, Eliasson; even Voelke claims no *indeterminist* freedom).
2. **A notion of *will/volition* at all?** — **SPLIT**: Voelke YES (full, not proto), Blackson "probably YES" (early Stoics likely too), Frede NO at the early Stoa / YES at Epictetus, Bobzien NO until later theological determinism.
3. **What does *eph' hēmin* mean?** — dominant reading **one-sided / causative** (Bobzien, with Inwood, Brennan, Salles, Eliasson converging); the two-sided/potestative indeterminist reading is held by the dominant camp to be read back anachronistically and, in antiquity, to belong only to Alexander.

**Debate-A agreement/disagreement lines (verified):**
- **Bobzien ⟂ Frede** = the fault-line. AGREE the early Stoa lacked free will; DISAGREE on what comes next: Frede (Epictetus *himself* = first free will, p.77) vs Bobzien (a *will*-faculty enters only later, in Platonist/Christian theological determinism, with Epictetus' *prohairesis* as an *influence*: 1998 p.173 / 2001 p.412). The clash is **real but reconstructed across the two books** (Frede 2011 postdates and partly responds to Bobzien; Bobzien 2001 does not name Frede as the chs. 6–8 antagonist).
- **Bobzien ≈ Inwood ≈ Brennan ≈ Salles ≈ Eliasson** converge on a one-sided/causative, autonomy-grounded (MR1/AA) Stoic responsibility, against the do-otherwise (MR2/CDO) reading. CAVEAT: Inwood (1985) predates and does not use Bobzien's one-sided/two-sided terminology; the convergence is *retrospective*, at the level of conclusions. Brennan and Eliasson *explicitly endorse* Bobzien's AA/MR1 distinction by name; Brennan *modifies* her historical story; Eliasson *breaks* with her on Alexander.
- **Blackson ⟂ Frede** contests the timeline from the *opposite* direction to Bobzien: not "did Epictetus invent it?" but "the *early* Stoics probably already had it."
- **Voelke** is largely **orthogonal** to Bobzien (targets the soul's volitive functions, not *eph' hēmin* semantics); his structural thesis is *compatible* with Frede on Epictetus as a high-water mark of will-language. Not simply "superseded."

A spectrum, not a binary: "genuine (if hedged) libertarian" → "libertarian-but-unestablished" → "seemingly / inadvertent, stops short" → "confused dead-end" → two flanks (one debunking, one rehabilitating).
