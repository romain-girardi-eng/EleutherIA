## HEADLINE THESIS

Sharples holds that **Alexander's own conception of moral responsibility is libertarian, not soft-determinist** — i.e. Alexander requires for responsibility an *unqualified, unrestricted* possibility of doing otherwise, not the Stoic "could have done otherwise if circumstances had differed". BUT Sharples simultaneously judges that Alexander **stipulates rather than establishes** this position and **"does not really solve"** the problem of combining libertarian freedom with a rational account of action (1983, pp. 21–22). In the 2008 retrospective he reframes the whole episode: the near-modern free-will problem that crystallises in Alexander's *De Fato* is, on the most economical reading, **a historical "accident"/coincidence** — the convergence of two independently-developing strands (RDS and PEA, defined below) — and he treats Bobzien's "inadvertent" thesis as broadly right while pressing back on its details.

## CLAIM 2 (CORE) — Alexander's conception of responsibility is **libertarian**, not soft-determinist

| field | content |
|---|---|
| **Page** | 1983, p. 9 (Introduction §1); restated p. 21 |
| **Verbatim anchor (EN), p. 9** | "nevertheless it can be shown that some thinkers, Alexander among them, have a libertarian rather than a soft-determinist conception of responsibility, and in such cases I have not hesitated to use expressions like 'freedom'." (1983, p. 9) |
| **Verbatim anchor (EN), p. 21** | "One crucial point that is however clear is that Alexander's own conception of responsibility is a libertarian one. He objects not just to determination of our actions by external causes alone, but to that resulting from a combination of internal and external factors". (1983, pp. 21–22) |
| **Methodological caveat (his own, p. 9)** | He flags that the Greek debate is conducted "entirely in terms of responsibility (*to eph' hēmin*) rather than of freedom or free will"; "libertarian"/"freedom" are **his** deliberately-applied modern categories, not Alexander's vocabulary. He also warns (p. 9, on *autexousion*, Bruns *f.* XIV 182.24) that even *to eph' hēmin* / *autexousion* "does not in itself necessarily have libertarian connotations." |
| **Ancient loci** | the "power to do or not do" / "to choose the opposite" formulae: *De Fato* 180.5–7; 180.20–21; 180.26–27; 181.3f.; 181.5–6; 189.10–11; 190.25; 199.9; 205.19–20; 211.32–33; *Mantissa* 172.11–12; 173.5–6; 174.11–12 Bruns (Greek **not-verbatim** here — transliterated loci only). Contrast object: the soft-determinist reading "what comes about through us" (*di' hēmōn*), *De Fato* XIII 181.14, XXXVIII 211.30. |
| **Agrees / contests** | **Against the soft-determinist reading of Alexander** (the position Sharples associates with D. Frede 1982/1984: see p. 9 n. 47 area and p. 21 n. 138). **With** the indeterminist/anti-determinist reading he shares with himself elsewhere (1987; 2001 pp. 559–561), Bobzien 1998a pp. 400–401, and Salles 1998. He explicitly opposes "libertarian" to both the "hard determinist" and the "soft determinist" (p. 9). |

> **Distinction (ASSERTS vs REPORTS):** the "soft determinism"/"hard determinism"/"libertarian" taxonomy is Sharples' **own analytic framework** (he credits the term "soft determinism" to William James, p. 9 n. 41), applied *as a tool* — he is explicit that the ancients lacked the free-will/freedom framing and used only *to eph' hēmin*. The claim that Alexander *is* a libertarian is Sharples' own scholarly **assertion**, hedged by the "I have not hesitated to use" wording.

## CLAIM 3 (CORE) — Alexander demands *unqualified, unrestricted* possibility — but **stipulates, does not establish**, and **"does not really solve"** the resulting problem

| field | content |
|---|---|
| **Page** | 1983, pp. 21–22 (Introduction §2) — the locus the 2008 article foot-references as "Sharples, 1983, p. 21-22" for the antideterminist reading |
| **Verbatim anchor (EN), p. 22** | "This shows that his repeated descriptions of responsibility in terms of the power or capacity for opposite courses of action are to be understood in terms of an unqualified, unrestricted possibility." (1983, p. 22) |
| **Verbatim anchor (EN), p. 22 — the load-bearing limitation** | "He stresses the connection between responsibility and reason, which shows that his libertarian conception of responsibility is not just one of arbitrary caprice; at the same time, **he faces very real difficulties in combining his libertarian position with an account of the rational element in human behaviour, and does not really solve it.**" (1983, p. 22; the "does not really solve" point is cross-referenced to the Commentary, p. 22 n. 150 → pp. 149, 163–4) |
| **Verbatim anchor (EN), p. 22 — where it bites hardest** | "This problem is perhaps most acute when human action is seen in the context of the universe as an ordered whole; this is so in *mantissa* XXII, with bizarre consequences – responsibility being identified with human weakness – but not, because of his piecemeal approach, in the *de fato*." (1983, p. 22) |
| **Ancient loci** | power-for-opposites formulae *De Fato* 180–211 Bruns (listed under Claim 2); the unsolved rational-element problem localised in ***Mantissa* XXII** (Bruns 170–172, esp. 172.9ff.) and *De Fato* XI 183.21–184.20; the contrast with Stoic/Neoplatonist "freedom = choosing the most rational course" at p. 22 n. 152 (Augustine *De civ. Dei* 22.30; Boethius *Cons.* 5 pr. 2.16,21; Plotinus 3.1.9.5, 3.1.10.4, 3.2.10) |
| **Agrees / contests** | **Converges with D. Frede and Donini** on the verdict that Alexander's libertarianism is asserted/stipulated rather than philosophically secured. **Note the convergence with Bobzien 1998b pp. 171–172** ("stops short of a concept of free will"; soul corporeal) — though Sharples (2008, p. 291 n. 1) says he suspects the **rationality** factor, not the corporeal-soul factor Bobzien stresses, is the more decisive reason the notion of *will* is still absent in Alexander. |

> **This is the key place where Sharples is NOT a simple "Alexander = libertarian hero" voice.** He grants the libertarian *intent* and *structure* but explicitly denies it is *successfully established*. Any map that lists Sharples only as "FOR (Alexander libertarian)" without the "unestablished / does not really solve" qualifier is incomplete.

## CLAIM 4 — Alexander's polemic distorts the Stoic position; his report of Stoicism "must be used with considerable caution"

| field | content |
|---|---|
| **Page** | 1983, pp. 20–21 |
| **Verbatim anchor (EN)** | "It follows, however, that his statements must be used with considerable caution as evidence for the Stoic position." (1983, p. 21) |
| **Supporting verbatim (EN), p. 20** | "from Alexander's libertarian point of view (see below) it is to stress that, for the determinists, given the character a man has, he must act in one way in one set of circumstances and in another in another. … every action must ultimately be traceable back to determining causes outside the agent." (1983, pp. 20–21) |
| **Ancient loci** | *De Fato* 180.5–7; XXXIV 206.19; XXXVI 208.9; *Mantissa* 174.4; identification of fate & necessity imputed to the Stoics *De Fato* X 176.23f., 177.5ff. (15), 178.6; cf. XXXIV 206.9ff. |
| **Agrees / contests** | **With A. A. Long (1970)** that Alexander isolates the determinism question from its Stoic providential context for his own philosophical purposes (p. 20 nn. 130–132). **Reports Donini's** rival thesis (Donini 1977 p. 183ff.) that Alexander attacks *a variety* of determinist positions (e.g. ch. IX vs the Megarian denial of possibility, *Metaph.* Θ 3) — Sharples grants the Aristotelian influence but **pushes back**: "the latter cert[a]inly gives his reader the impression that he is attacking a single set of opponents" (p. 21). |

## CLAIM 5 — Aristotle himself operates with "basically libertarian assumptions" (but was not arguing against a determinist system)

| field | content |
|---|---|

| **Verbatim anchor (EN)** | "It seems that he is operating with basically libertarian assumptions, starting from the position that responsibility involves freedom to choose between different courses of action, and dealing with difficulties arising from the determination of action by character only as a subordinate issue." ## CLAIM 6 (2008) — The historiographical "accident": the near-modern problem in Alexander is a **coincidence** of two independent strands (RDS + PEA)

| field | content |
|---|---|
| **Page** | 2008, pp. 296–301 (sections IV–VI; conclusion p. 301) |
| **Definitions (Sharples' abbreviations)** | **PA** = "possibilité d'alternative" (possibility of alternatives, ambiguous between compatibilist and indeterminist). **PEA** = "possibilité égale pour les deux termes de l'alternative" (equal possibility for both branches), arising *within Peripatetic Aristotle-exegesis* on the threefold division of the contingent. **RDS** = "rejet de la possibilité de l'alternative" — a **reformulated Stoic determinism** ("from a given situation, one and only one situation can follow"), which Sharples (with Bobzien & Long) attributes to **Philopator** (late 1st / early 2nd c. CE). |
| **Verbatim anchor (FR), p. 301 — the thesis statement** | "La possibilité restante est que RDS et PEA soient des développements indépendants, et que le fait qu'ils se soient développés vers la même époque et qu'Alexandre ait été capable … de les associer pour formuler le problème du déterminisme et de la responsabilité d'une manière anticipant les discussions modernes relève, en quelque sorte, de la coïncidence." (2008, p. 301) — *"The remaining possibility is that RDS and PEA are independent developments, and that their developing around the same time and Alexander's being able to associate them so as to formulate the problem of determinism and responsibility in a way anticipating modern discussions is, in a sense, a matter of coincidence."* |
| **Verbatim anchor (FR), p. 301 — the title's payoff** | "que la question du déterminisme se soit développée sous l'effet d'une coïncidence, voilà qui apparaîtra sans doute un peu, au bout du compte, comme une ironie de l'histoire." (2008, p. 301) — *"that the determinism question developed through a coincidence will no doubt look, in the end, somewhat like an irony of history."* |
| **Verbatim anchor (FR), p. 294 — Alexander's Stoic terms, with one crucial difference** | "l'approche du problème de l'agir responsable dans le *De fato* se fait en des termes essentiellement stoïciens, malgré – à mon avis – une différence cruciale : Alexandre refuse de reconnaître que la position déterministe nous autorise à rejeter les impressions. Elle l'autorisait, pour les stoïciens, mais seulement d'une manière prédéterminée ; et pour le partisan de la liberté qu'était Alexandre, cela revenait à ne rien autoriser du tout." (2008, p. 294) |
| **Ancient loci** | "Argument A" (compatibilist argument attributed by Nemesius to Chrysippus AND Philopator) = *De Fato* ch. 13 (Bruns) ≈ Nemesius *De nat. hom.* 34, 102.8–103.10 Morani; Alexander *In An. Pr.* I.13 162.1–163.23; *In Top.* 177.22–27; *De Fato* ch. 26 (attack on PA via influence of character on action). |
| **Agrees / contests** | **Broadly endorses Bobzien 1998a/1998b** (the "inadvertent conception" thesis) as the framework, calling her critique of rendering *to eph' hēmin* as "free-will" "on ne peut plus juste" (entirely right) (2008, p. 291 n. 1). **But pushes back on three points:** (i) he sides with **Brennan 2001/2005** that Bobzien's external-vs-internal-factors distinction is better recast as a *shifting boundary of the self* (Brennan, 2001 pp. 282–286) and that Bobzien "aille trop loin" (goes too far) in her methodological caution (2008, p. 297–298); (ii) he is "plus probable … de voir en RDS la conséquence de PEA, que l'inverse" — i.e. **RDS is more likely the consequence of PEA than vice-versa** (2008, p. 300), against Bobzien's main line that the Stoics were responding to PEA; (iii) he remains "enclin à croire" that the underlying conception of two-sided possibility was already present in the **Epicurean *clinamen*** and in **Carneades** (2008, p. 300 n. 4 — *pace* Bobzien 2000), so the strand is older than Bobzien allows. He also reports **Boys-Stones 2007** (Middle Platonist PEA was compatibilist, not antideterminist) and **Salles 2005** as siding with him on regularity in Chrysippus. |

> **Distinction (ASSERTS vs REPORTS):** in the 2008 article Sharples spends most of §III–V **reporting and weighing Bobzien's** reconstruction; his **own** committed claims are (a) the "coincidence/accident" reading of the convergence, (b) the RDS-from-PEA priority, (c) the older-than-Bobzien-allows pedigree of two-sided possibility (Epicurus, Carneades), and (d) the methodological self-critique of his 1983 "Laplacian" framing.

## SUPPORTING / CONTEXT CLAIMS (briefer)

- **C7 — *to eph' hēmin* is shared vocabulary, not a reliable guide to position.** 1983, p. 9 (verbatim): "The Greek *to eph' hēmin*, 'what depends on us', like the English 'responsibility', was used both by libertarians and by soft determinists, though they differed as to what it involved; thus the occurrence of the expression is not a safe guide to the type of position involved." → bears on **Debate D**. Ancient loci: *De Fato* passim; *autexousion* at *f.* XIV 182.24.
- **C8 — Chrysippus is a "soft determinist."** 1983, p. 9 (verbatim): "His position was thus one of 'soft determinism', as opposed on the one hand to that of the 'hard determinist' … and on the other to that of the libertarian". Ancient loci: cylinder analogy, *De Fato* 181.13ff. ≈ Cicero *De Fato* 39–43, Gellius *NA* 7.2; *argos logos* (Lazy Argument), Cicero *De Fato* 28–30. → bears on **Debate A**. (This is the standard taxonomy, asserted as Sharples' analytic reading, term credited to W. James.)
- **C9 — The notion of *will* is still future in Alexander.** 2008, p. 291 n. 1 (reporting+endorsing Bobzien 1998b pp. 171–172) but redirecting the explanation: "Je soupçonne le second facteur [la rationalité] d'être plus décisif que le premier [l'âme corporelle]." Sharples' own emphasis on **rationality** as why *will* has not yet split off from intellect. → bears on **Debate B** (origins of will) and Debate C.

## FLAGS AGAINST THE QUICK-PASS MAP (`data/goals/g5/debate_map.md`)

The quick-pass map's Debate-C rows for Sharples 1983 are **substantially accurate** and well-anchored. Two refinements:

1. **Row "Sharples 1983 — FOR (hedged category)", loci & page.** The map gives "*De Fato* 181.5; 180.26–8; 199.9; 211.32 Bruns | 9". **Verified correct.** p. 9 is exactly where Sharples states the libertarian-vs-soft-determinist verdict and the "I have not hesitated to use 'freedom'" caveat. The four Bruns loci are all genuine power-for-opposites passages (181.5, 180.26–8 = "choose the opposite"; 199.9 = "do or not do"; 211.32–33 = "do the opposite"). **No error.**

2. **Row "Sharples 1983 — FOR but unestablished", page 22.** The map's paraphrase ("'unqualified, unrestricted possibility', yet 'faces very real difficulties … and does not really solve it' (*Mantissa* XXII)") is **verbatim-accurate to p. 22.** **No error.** Minor precision: the "*Mantissa* XXII" tag in the map should read as the locus where the problem is *most acute* (responsibility = human weakness), per Sharples p. 22; the "does not really solve it" verdict is general (cross-ref Commentary pp. 149, 163–4), not confined to *Mantissa* XXII. Worth tightening but not a misrepresentation.

3. **Gap, not error — the 2008 "accident" thesis is absent from the map.** The map captures only the 1983 stance. Sharples' mature, signed position (Debate-C-relevant) is the **"accident/coincidence of determinism"** thesis (Claim 6) and his **self-critique** of the 1983 "Laplacian" framing. If the map is meant to represent Sharples' considered view, it currently **under-represents** him: it omits (a) that he later half-recants the "retrospective history" method, (b) that he broadly **endorses Bobzien's "inadvertent" framework** while contesting RDS↔PEA priority, and (c) that he posits the two-sided-possibility strand already in **Epicurus and Carneades** (*pace* Bobzien). Recommend adding a Sharples-2008 row.

No factual misrepresentation of Sharples found in the quick-pass map; only an under-coverage of his 2008 retrospective.
