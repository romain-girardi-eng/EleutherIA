## HEADLINE THESIS

Frede argues that "a free will" is **a technical, philosophical notion with an identifiable historical origin**, not an ordinary belief humans have always held. Neither Plato nor Aristotle had it. It **first arises in late Stoicism in the first century AD and is clearly present in Epictetus**; Platonists, Peripatetics (esp. Alexander), and then Christians adopt and modify it. The Christian appropriation (from Justin Martyr onward, systematically in Origen, then Augustine) introduces **no radically new notion**: it relies on a basically Stoic doctrine of freedom, and Augustine in fact adheres to the *Stoic* position more closely than Origen does. This **contests Dihle 1982** (who made Augustine the "inventor of our modern notion of will") — but Frede explicitly says his **aim is "completely different from Dihle's"** (purely historical, no favored modern notion).

## ASSERTS vs REPORTS — discipline notes

- **Frede ASSERTS** (his own positions): Claims 1–13 above are Frede's own. The labels "free will", "the will", *autexousion*, "Stoic", "Platonist" are used by Frede in his own voice as historical-analytical categories — but note he is at pains (pp. 6–9) to say he does **not** endorse any particular *modern* notion; the schema is "abstracted from those texts which explicitly talk of a will".
- **"Compatibilism / libertarian"**: Frede does **not** lean on the modern term "compatibilism"; his Stoic free will is one in which "a choice might be no less free for having a perfectly good explanation in terms of antecedent causes" (p. 100) — compatibilist *in substance* but he states it in his own descriptive terms. The word **"libertarian" is not Frede's framing** of his own view; the "open alternatives in the very same circumstances" conception is what he attributes to **Alexander** and judges incoherent (Claim 12). Any "libertarian" label on Frede's positive view would misrepresent him.
- **Frede REPORTS others**: Dihle's thesis ("Augustine inventor of our modern notion of will", quoted by the editor p. 179 from Dihle p. 144); Ross's "plain man's belief" (p. 3); Bobzien's catalogue of *autexousion* uses (ch. 5 n. 7); Marcellus of Ancyra's and Eusebius's reading of Origen's *Peri archōn* title (p. 112). These are reported, not endorsed wholesale (he endorses parts of Dihle's *learning*, rejects his *thesis*).
