# Debate A — VERIFIED FOR/AGAINST map

**Question:** Did the early Stoa have a notion of *free will*, and what is *to eph' hēmin*?

Adversarial re-verification of `data/goals/g5/debate_map.md` (Debate A) against the deep-read dossiers in `data/goals/g5_deep/` and, for the highest-stakes anchors, against the source files themselves. Method: default to DOUBT; for every scholar, confirm the cited stance is genuinely held on the cited page; distinguish ASSERT from REPORT; catch misattribution, overstatement, wrong locus, page slips.

**Integrity:** every modern label ("free will", "libertarian", "compatibilism", "soft determinism", "invention of the will", "freedom to do otherwise") is ATTRIBUTED to the named scholar, never asserted here as historical fact. Original-language anchors are given with the scholar's own English; French quotes (Voelke) are kept in French + English, never as a French *substitute* for an English rendering. Greek transcriptions flagged where the source OCR/betacode is degraded.

The question is asked three ways by the literature, and the answers split differently on each:
1. **Did the early Stoa have a *libertarian / indeterminist* free will?** — near-unanimous **NO** (Bobzien, Inwood, Brennan, Salles, Frede, Eliasson; even Voelke does not claim *indeterminist* freedom).
2. **Did the early Stoa have a notion of *will / volition* at all?** — **split**: Voelke YES (full, not proto), Blackson "probably YES" (early Stoics likely had a *notion of a will* under his reading), Frede NO (a *will* emerges only in late Stoicism / Epictetus), Bobzien NO (a faculty of *will* enters only later, in theological determinism, via Epictetus' *prohairesis* as influence).
3. **What does *eph' hēmin* mean?** — dominant reading: **one-sided / causative** (Bobzien, with Inwood, Brennan, Salles, Eliasson converging); the two-sided / potestative indeterminist reading is held by the dominant camp to be read back anachronistically (and to belong, in antiquity, only to Alexander).

## Agreement / disagreement lines (verified)

- **Bobzien ⟂ Frede** — the fault-line. **AGREE** the early Stoa lacked free will. **DISAGREE** on what comes next: Frede (Epictetus *himself* = first free will, asserted p. 77) vs Bobzien (a *will*-faculty enters only later, in Platonist/Christian theological determinism, with Epictetus' *prohairesis* as an *influence*, 1998 p. 173 / 2001 p. 412). The clash is **real but reconstructed across the two books** — Frede 2011 postdates and partly responds to Bobzien; Bobzien 2001 does not name Frede as the live antagonist of chs. 6–8.
- **Bobzien ≈ Inwood ≈ Brennan ≈ Salles ≈ Eliasson** — converge on a one-sided/causative, autonomy-grounded (MR1 / AA) Stoic responsibility, against the do-otherwise (MR2 / CDO) reading. **Caveat (verified):** Inwood (1985) predates Bobzien's one-sided/two-sided *terminology* and does not use it; the convergence is *retrospective*. Brennan and Eliasson *explicitly endorse* Bobzien's AA/MR1 distinction by name; Brennan *modifies* her historical story; Eliasson *endorses* her on Chrysippus but *breaks with* her on Alexander.
- **Salles ⟂ Frede** — Salles reads Epictetus as continuous-compatibilist (general not specific capacity), implicitly against any "invention of free will." The 2005 book does **not** address Frede 2011 (later); the explicit Salles-contra-Frede polemic is the 2014 Destrée/Salles/Zingano essay (correctly so cited in the quick-pass).
- **Blackson ⟂ Frede** — directly contests Frede's timeline from a direction the quick-pass map never captured: not "did Epictetus invent it?" but "the *early* Stoics probably already had it," making Frede wrong on *both* ends under Blackson's E-reading.
- **Voelke vs the later debate** — Voelke's *positive* thesis (reason = will; *tonos* as primordial will) is largely **orthogonal** to Bobzien, who targets *eph' hēmin* semantics and the do-otherwise problem, not the internal theory of the soul's volitive functions. Voelke is not simply "superseded"; his structural claim is *compatible* with Frede on Epictetus as a high-water mark of will-language.

## Corrections to the quick-pass map (`data/goals/g5/debate_map.md`, Debate A)

No outright **misattribution** of a position to the wrong scholar was found in Debate A. The errors caught are: page/locus slips, one citation-hygiene error recurring across rows, two overstatement/understatement issues, two significant omissions, and one omitted scholar.

1. **Bobzien 1998 — page span is a line-number/page conflation (citation hygiene).** Map row gives "133–142, 187–189, **375–412**." The "**375–412**" range is **.md line numbers**, not printed Phronesis pages; the causative/potestative section it points to sits on printed **pp. 139–142**. The same line-number-as-page slip recurs in the Debate D rows ("375–412", "1504–1611"). **Fix:** relabel as printed pages (causative/potestative = 139–142; ἐξουσία section = 163–167). *Content not affected.* (Self-flagged in `read_bobzien_1998.md`; confirmed.)

2. **Bobzien 2001 — "*eph' hēmin* = *di' hēmōn*" is an imprecise gloss.** Bobzien's one-sided/causative term for Chrysippus is **παρ' ἡμᾶς / ἐξ ἡμῶν** (with γίγνεσθαι), not literally *di' hēmōn*. The two-sided/potestative pair is **ἐφ' ἡμῖν (with εἶναι)**. **Fix:** state the contrast as παρ' ἡμᾶς (one-sided) vs ἐφ' ἡμῖν (two-sided); tighten the definition locus to **pp. 281–285** (map folds it into "276–282"). Overstatement risk: low; refinement only.

3. **Inwood 1985 — wrong page ranges + a mis-association.** (a) Cleanthes *Hymn* is **pp. 69–71**, not "66–70"; Alexander *eph' hēmin* discussion is **pp. 88–91**, not "88–89." (b) The map clusters Inwood with the Bobzien-style *one-sided/causative eph' hēmin* argument. **Inwood does not make that semantic argument** — he argues from the *criterion of responsibility* (assent vs do-otherwise), not from the etymological valence of the phrase. **Fix:** correct pages; flag that Inwood's convergence with Bobzien is on the *responsibility criterion*, not on the *eph' hēmin* semantics (which he never analyses). Mild overstatement caught.

4. **Brennan 2005 — two substantive OMISSIONS + one misleading locus.** (a) The map records only Brennan's compatibilism; it OMITS his explicit verdict that Chrysippus' co-fated reply to the **Lazy Argument is "a surprising and disappointing failure"** (p. 285) — Brennan holds Chrysippus is a coherent compatibilist *and* fails on this specific objection. (b) The map OMITS the **"shrinking of the self"** developmental thesis (pp. 292–294), Brennan's main original contribution. (c) Listing "**Idle Argument**" in the *contested-locus* column alongside the cylinder is **misleading**: ch. 16 (Lazy/Idle Argument) is where Brennan finds Chrysippus *wanting*, not where he grounds the compatibilist reading (ch. 15, cylinder). The fuller AA/CDO grounding is pp. 288–294, not just 255–257. **Fix:** add omissions (a),(b); move the Idle Argument out of the support column or mark it as the *failure* locus.

5. **Salles 2005 — "three compatibilist theories" needs the T₂ qualification.** Salles identifies T₁, T₂, T₃, but **argues T₂ is a purely modal/metaphysical theory, NOT a responsibility/compatibilist theory.** "Three compatibilist theories" unqualified misstates the architecture: only **T₁ and T₃** are responsibility theories. Also: the **Idle/Lazy Argument** in Salles is *De Fato* **28–29** (with confatalia at 30), and Salles' Epictetus chapter is about *prohairesis* / the Normative Argument, **not** *eph' hēmin* semantics — the map's "Epictetus on *eph' hēmin*" suggests a Bobzien-style engagement Salles does not undertake. **Fix:** qualify T₂; correct the Idle-Argument locus; re-label the Epictetus locus.

6. **Voelke 1973 — two errors in the map's own annotation.** (a) "**image-scan PDF, no OCR; quote null**" is **FALSE** — the PDF is OCR-readable throughout and verbatim French is fully extractable (the dossier transcribes ~10 passages). (b) "**proto**" **understates** Voelke: he argues for a *full, worked-out* *idée de volonté* in the early Stoa, not an anticipation; "proto" assimilates him to a minimalist/developmental position he explicitly rejects (his target is Dyroff's "intellectualism ignoring will"). **Fix:** remove the "no OCR / quote null" note; drop "proto" → "FOR volition (full, not proto)."

7. **Frede 2011 — page-anchor hygiene (summary line-numbers ≠ printed pages).** The map's Debate-A Frede page cite "**ch. 2–6, esp. 2770–2773**" uses the `Frede_2011.summary.md` **line offsets**, not printed pages. The "first actual notion of a free will" sentence is printed **p. 77** (verified in source, txt line 2739). **Fix:** cite printed **p. 77** for the headline claim; ch. 3 (pp. 44–46) for the will-from-assent; pp. 74–75 for *autexousion*/Justin. Also worth making explicit in the map: **"libertarian" is not Frede's label for his own positive view** — he assigns the incoherent open-alternatives notion to Alexander (pp. 100–101); a "libertarian" tag on Frede's Stoic free will would misrepresent him.

8. **Blackson 2025 — OMITTED ENTIRELY from the map.** The quick-pass Debate-A table has no Blackson row, yet his article directly contests the Frede timeline the map foregrounds. **Fix:** add Blackson as a "FOR (early-Stoa, contra Frede)" row, flagged **WEAK** as a consensus claim because his own conclusion is explicitly hedged ("probably," limited to best-known passages). This is the single most consequential gap in the quick-pass for Debate A: it shows Frede may be wrong on the early Stoics in the direction *opposite* to what the map implies.

### ASSERT-vs-REPORT check (no confusions found, two clarifications)

- **Frede / Augustine.** Frede *asserts* his own positive thesis (Epictetus = first free will; Augustine more Stoic than Origen) and *reports* Dihle's "Augustine invented our modern notion of will" only to reject it. The map keeps this straight. (Separately, the **Debate B** Kahn-node flag — that a KG `validity_assessment` mis-states *Frede* as holding the will was "already present in Aristotle" — is a genuine error: Frede asserts the **opposite** (no will in Aristotle, p. 28); the "already in Aristotle" view is Irwin/Kenny's. This is a Debate-B item, correctly flagged there, not a Debate-A error.)
- **Brennan / "soft determinist."** Correctly attributed as Brennan's *own modern analytical label*, not a Stoic self-description — no assert/report confusion.
- **Inwood / Origen.** Clarification (not an error): Inwood deploys Origen *De Princ.* III.1 as a *Stoic doxographic witness*, NOT as an independent philosopher of free will. Any reading of the map that treats Inwood's Origen use as engagement with *Origen's own* position would be mistaken.

## Net verdict

The quick-pass Debate-A map is **substantively sound on every scholar's stance** — no position is assigned to the wrong scholar, and no modern label is asserted as historical fact (all stay attributed). The defects are: (1) recurring line-number-as-page citation slips (Bobzien, Frede); (2) imprecise page ranges (Inwood); (3) two understatements/overstatements (Voelke "proto" + false "no OCR"; Inwood mis-associated with the *eph' hēmin* semantic argument); (4) two real omissions of Brennan's positions (Lazy-Argument failure; self-shrinkage) plus a misleading Idle-Argument locus shared with Salles; (5) the T₂ "compatibilist" over-count in Salles; and (6) the wholesale omission of **Blackson 2025**, the one source that pressures Frede's timeline from the early-Stoa side. All high-stakes verbatim anchors (Frede p. 77; Bobzien 1998 pp. 135/139/142; Salles xiv) were re-confirmed against the source files.
