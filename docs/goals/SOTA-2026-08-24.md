# EleutherIA SOTA Program — evidence, engine, atlas and discovery

**Status:** active, blocking gates open  
**Started:** 2026-08-24  
**North star:** a light-dominant, immersive intellectual atlas whose claims are
traceable to exact ancient loci and printed secondary pages, whose GraphRAG
fails closed, and whose largest graph interactions feel immediate.

This is a living execution program, not a completion claim. The machine-readable
source of truth is `data/goals/sota/`. “Exhaustive” means operationally
exhaustive as of a dated search closure; “zero error” means zero known factual
debt after all declared gates and human sign-off.

## 2. Non-negotiable release contract

A release may be called SOTA only when all of the following hold on the same
immutable release hash:

1. **Source identity:** each ancient work, witness, edition, translation,
   secondary publication and manifestation has a stable identity and authority
   record where one exists.
2. **Atomic evidence:** each factual or interpretive proposition points to an
   ancient locus or printed secondary page; reconstruction and attestation are
   distinct types.
3. **Text integrity:** original, translation, editorial synthesis, fragment,
   testimonium and retroversion can never be silently substituted.
4. **Independent review:** every published evidence unit has a primary review,
   a genuinely independent review and an adversarial refutation pass.
5. **Fail-closed answers:** unresolved, rejected, missing or weak evidence
   prevents publication and cache admission.
6. **Zero known debt:** every factual-risk issue is resolved or adjudicated;
   the snapshot-integrity baseline is empty.
7. **Reproducibility:** evidence-bearing artifacts have SHA-256,
   edition/manifestation identity, page maps and declared rights.
8. **Human sign-off:** two named scholars from distinct review groups sign the
   exact KG, corpus, registry, bibliography and runtime release hashes.

The eleven formal gates are defined in `data/goals/sota/exit_gates.json`.

## 3. Product architecture: two independent mode systems

“Multiple modes” applies at two layers and must not create duplicate state.

### 3.1 Knowledge-atlas modes

All three projections share selection, filters, comparison set, evidence
thread, annotations, history and URL state.

#### Atlas

- Default language: warm parchment, ink, terracotta, amber, olive and
  restrained Aegean blue.
- Initial view: 100–300 anchors or community supernodes, never the raw full
  graph.
- Semantic zoom reveals 1–3k nodes, ego neighborhoods and exact evidence paths.
- “Evidence Flight” may shift the selected path into a deeper Aegean field for
  cinematic focus; darkness is a local state, not the global theme.
- Signature interaction: **Evidence Thread** — claim → ancient locus →
  edition/translation → modern interpretation → dispute.

#### Chronos

- The same IDs become chronological lanes: composition, witness,
  transmission, translation, reception and controversy.
- Time is a first-class filter, not node opacity lost during compaction.
- Transmission gaps, indirect witnesses and uncertain dates are explicit.
- Selection in Atlas opens the same dossier in Chronos without refetching.

#### Scholar

- Dense, light research workstation with resizable panes.
- Parallel original/translation/variant witnesses; edition and translator
  always visible.
- Claim ledger, page/locus evidence, provenance, dispute comparison, notes and
  BibTeX/RIS/CSV/GraphML/JSON-LD export.
- Keyboard-complete relational alternative to the WebGL/canvas view.
- Up to four entities/claims compared without losing primary selection.

### 3.2 GraphRAG modes

These are query-depth modes, separate from visual projection.

| Mode | Purpose | Hard target |
|---|---|---|
| Quick | Exact identity, locus, definition and short factual questions | p95 < 5 s; structured answer when prose is unnecessary |
| Scholar | Standard research question with attributed positions and exact citations | p95 < 45 s; < 80k tokens; complete claim ledger |
| Deep | Exhaustive controversy/source investigation | Durable async job; resumable, cancellable, budgeted; not tied to SSE lifetime |

All modes produce the same immutable `EvidenceUnit` contract. A boolean
`verified` is forbidden as a compression of different states; use at least
`resolved`, `text_exact`, `entailed`, `source_verified`,
`independently_reviewed` and `human_reviewed`.

## 4. Target data plane for AAA-level interaction

The canvas must never download descriptions and scholarly prose merely to draw
points.

### 4.1 Offline release build

For each release, precompute:

- canonical ID and alias tables;
- interned dictionaries and typed-array node/edge tables;
- CSR adjacency in both directions;
- community hierarchy and stable L0/L1/L2 layouts;
- per-mode coordinates for Atlas and Chronos;
- label salience, degree, authority and evidence-risk flags;
- label/transliteration/Greek-Latin lemma/identifier search indices;
- immutable content hashes and release manifest.

Ship a compact initial graph under 2 MB Brotli. Fetch descriptions, passages,
scholarship and source dossiers after selection. Use immutable CDN paths, ETag
and content hashes.

### 4.2 Runtime ownership

A Worker or SharedWorker owns parsing, indices, filters, path queries and
adjacency. It transfers typed buffers zero-copy. React owns interface state;
the renderer owns camera and drawing; neither rebuilds the graph on every tab
or filter change.

`GraphWorkspaceStore` contains:

```text
mode
releaseId
primarySelection
compareIds[0..4]
evidenceThread
filters + timeWindow
cameraByMode
panels
history undo/redo
notes + exports
adaptiveQuality
```

The state is serializable as a permalink. Meaningful navigation pushes browser
history; transient camera motion replaces it.

### 4.3 Adaptive quality

Degrade decoration before evidence:

1. reduce device-pixel ratio;
2. reduce edge curvature, bloom and particles;
3. aggregate distant edges;
4. reduce nonessential labels;
5. preserve selected evidence path, provenance badges and essential labels.

Use an EWMA of frame time. Enable 120 Hz only after two seconds below the
8.3 ms budget. Fall back to a 2D relational/table view on low memory or WebGL
loss.

## 5. Performance budgets

### Interaction

- Mid-range desktop/mobile: p95 frame ≤ 16.7 ms, <1% dropped frames at 60 Hz.
- High-end stretch: p95 frame ≤ 8.3 ms, <3% dropped frames at 120 Hz.
- Search/select/mode-switch UI response: p95 < 100 ms.
- Worker filtering on the 120k-edge fixture: < 250 ms.
- Camera settle after a deliberate mode change: < 250 ms.
- No unexplained long task > 50 ms.

### Loading and memory

- Atlas usable: < 2.5 s Fast 4G desktop; < 4 s mid-range Android.
- Non-blocking branded intro: ≤ 600 ms and skipped when data is ready.
- Initial compact graph: ≤ 2 MB Brotli.
- Mode switch: no graph refetch.
- Graph heap: < 100 MB desktop, < 60 MB mobile.

### Web quality

- INP < 200 ms, LCP < 2.5 s, CLS < 0.1 at p75 after real-user measurement.
- WCAG 2.2 AA, including non-color node-type encoding and full keyboard
  access to the evidence graph.

## 6. GraphRAG target architecture

### 6.1 Evidence first

The synthesis model receives only immutable, source-scoped evidence units:

```text
evidence_id + release_id
claimable_text
source_kind
kg_node_id + corpus_passage_id or publication_id
CTS/locus or printed page
edition/witness/translator
text_sha256 + source_sha256
passage_role + translation_type
citability + review states
```

For secondary literature, build a real page-level corpus. A publication
description is not evidence for a page-specific scholarly claim.

### 6.2 Retrieval

Run independent retrieval legs in parallel:

1. exact identifier/CTS/DOI lookup;
2. lexical + lemmatic original-language search;
3. canonical entity/alias resolution;
4. relation-whitelisted graph patterns;
5. page-level secondary retrieval;
6. controversy and counter-evidence retrieval.

Fuse and rerank for entailment, source authority, diversity and risk. Degree
may never overwhelm lexical relevance. Precompute or cache local PPR and
neighborhoods by release and seed.

### 6.3 Synthesis and gates

Final prose and final ledger are generated together, then verified after the
last referee revision. Output and cache admission require:

- no parser error;
- no `REJECTED`, `MISSING` or `WEAK` citation;
- quote containment and exact source refetch;
- content, completeness, anti-anachronism and scholar-fidelity gates;
- one-to-one correspondence between prose claims and ledger;
- explicit disclosure for a non-original or machine translation;
- cache key including release hash, prompts, gates and model policy.

If verification infrastructure fails, return a typed “verification unavailable”
state, never the draft as a verified scholarly answer.

Implemented in the first P0 wave: one shared post-referee publication verdict
now governs `ScholarlyAgent`, the GraphRAG service, SSE and both caches. A
partial audit or any `WEAK`, `REJECTED`, `MISSING`, parser error, abort or
verifier exception withholds prose/citations/ledger and prevents cache entry.

Implemented in the second P0 wave: ancient passage slugs resolve uniquely to
fresh corpus text rather than KG descriptions; modern positions resolve only
through a private page-level evidence store keyed by publication and reviewed
page, with source/text hashes and fail-closed rights/review requirements. The
schema, ingestion and fetcher are implemented and tested, but no remote
migration or real secondary-page backfill is claimed.
