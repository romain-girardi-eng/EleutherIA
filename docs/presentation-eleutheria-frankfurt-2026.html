Core principle

Zero hallucination

Generative AI never composes Greek or Latin. Every citation points to a real text, verified in the database, with a confidence score.

✕ FORBIDDEN
  • Generating Greek or Latin
  • Reconstructing a lost text
  • Completing a fragmentary quotation
  • Translating a modern idea into an ancient language
✓ REQUIRED
  • Query the database before quoting
  • Verify that the passage exists
  • Include the attribution (CTS URN)
  • When in doubt, paraphrase in English

The challenge

"How does Chrysippus's cylinder argument respond to Carneades's critique of Stoic determinism, and how does Bobzien reconstruct that exchange?"

Answering it requires reasoning across the graph, retrieving the relevant passages, and synthesizing an answer grounded in the sources.

Agentic GraphRAG in practice

The right mental model

An agentic system is not an LLM left alone with the corpus. It is an organized workflow, where each layer plays a precise role in the inquiry, the reading and the checking.

Python · orchestration layer

The conductor

Ranks the passages, builds the file, budgets the tokens, checks sentence by sentence. The LLM writes; Python decides what to give it and validates what it produces.

Language model

The analyst

It forms hypotheses, chooses the relevant sections, produces reading notes and drafts the synthesis.

PostgreSQL + tree index

The library

This layer gives access to the texts, the relations, the citations and the hierarchical structure of the works.

Research graph

The living notebook

It keeps the facets of the question, the reading decisions, the works selected and the counter-evidence.

Programmatic verification

The critical secretary

It checks the answer sentence by sentence and refuses any claim that is not actually supported by the sources.

So the agent is not just the model. It is the whole system that organizes the inquiry, reads the right texts, keeps a trace of its reasoning and refuses what it cannot prove.

Why it matters

From a chatbot to a research instrument

For a scholar, an answer is not enough. You must be able to reconstruct the inquiry: which sources were opened, which leads were discarded, where the counter-evidence lies and which passages the synthesis rests on.

auditability reproducibility visible counter-evidence easier critical reading

Act VI · Research & FAIR

Retrieving the sources

Full text

PostgreSQL GIN + ts_rank. Exact word matching.

plainto_tsquery('simple', 'fate')
Lemmatic

Morphological search over pre-indexed Greek and Latin lemmas.

morphology @> '{"l": "εἱμαρμένη"}'
Semantic

KG citations, lemmas and full text. Vectorless retrieval.

sqlstrategy.search("libre arbitre")
Full text + Lemmatic + Semantic → RRF fusion (k=60)

Act VIII · Conclusion

What this changes

Textual grounding

Every claim is anchored in a verified ancient text. No speculation, no fabrication.

Visible connections

The graph makes visible intellectual networks that stay invisible in a linear reading of the texts.

AI in the service of the sources

The AI reasons over the sources — it does not replace them. The scholar remains in charge of interpretation.

Perspectives

Corpus expansion

Ingesting new works via the Scaife connector (Perseus Digital Library 5.0)

DH interoperability

Integration with existing digital-humanities tools (TEI, Perseids, Alpheios)

Specialized agents

Agents for bibliographic search and automated fact-checking

Community contributions

Opening the graph to corrections and enrichments by researchers

A portable infrastructure

The architecture (corpus + graph + GraphRAG) adapts to other corpora and other questions.

Critical editions

CREHE — Ausonius, Life of Saint Honoratus, Constantine VII. A graph of stemmatic relations between manuscripts and variants.

Urban archaeology

FABUR — Pompeii, medieval land registers. Linking construction phases, legal texts and scholarly interpretations.

Funerary transitions

TRANSFUN (M. Lauwers) — Inscriptions, charters, hagiography. Unifying text and archaeology to trace the Christianization of necropoles.

Zoological knowledge

Zoomathia (EPICS) — Bestiaries, Aristotle–Pliny–Albert the Great. Already in Linked Data; GraphRAG would add reasoning over the sources.
